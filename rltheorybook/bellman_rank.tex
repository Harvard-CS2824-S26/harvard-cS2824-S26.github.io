\chapter{Generalization with Bounded Bellman Rank}
\label{chap:Bellman_rank}

In the tabular setting, statistical efficiency — and strategic
exploration with UCBVI — comes from
directly estimating a finite transition table.  This idea appears explicitly in the generative-model
analysis of Chapter~\ref{chap:generative}, and in the online episodic setting of
Chapter~\ref{chap:tabular_exploration}, where optimism drives the learner to visit uncertain
state--action pairs. 

Moving beyond tabular representations requires structural assumptions.
Chapter~\ref{chap:linear_features} studied linear function
approximation under closure conditions such as linear Bellman
completeness, which reduce dynamic programming to regression; however,
the clean learnability results there were obtained under a
generative-model interface, and therefore sidestepped the exploration
problem.  Chapter~\ref{chap:linear_MDPs} imposed a stronger transition
model that makes the Bellman-relevant predictions linear, enabling
optimistic exploration and regret guarantees that scale polynomially
in the feature dimension.

This chapter develops a more general lens for \emph{generalization} in
reinforcement learning.  We seek conditions under which a learner can
find a near-optimal policy using a number of episodes that scales with an intrinsic complexity parameter rather than with $|\Scal|$ (and sometimes rather than with
$|\Acal|$).  The key object is the \emph{average Bellman error} of one hypothesis evaluated under the
state distributions induced by another.  When these average Bellman
errors admit a low-rank factorization 
(the \emph{Bellman rank} condition), trajectory data collected under
one roll-in policy can be reused to test many candidate hypotheses, yielding Probably Approximately Correct guarantees in large state spaces.

Of central importance is the data interface: here the learner has only
episodic interaction and must explore in order to obtain informative
data. Our focus is therefore on the \emph{PAC-RL} question — how to
return an $\epsilon$-optimal policy with high probability from
trajectory access alone — which is typically more challenging than
planning with a simulator. This is a weaker requirement than regret:
we aim to output a single good policy, rather than control cumulative
suboptimality online. We discuss the relationship between PAC and
regret herein. 

\section{Setting}
\label{sec:br_setting}

We work in an episodic finite-horizon MDP with horizon $H$, state space $\Scal$, action space $\Acal$,
and a fixed start state $s_0$.\footnote{Extending to an initial distribution $\mu$ is straightforward; we
use a fixed $s_0$ to keep notation light.}
Rewards satisfy $r_h(s,a)\in[0,1]$.

For any nonstationary policy $\pi=\{\pi_h\}_{h=0}^{H-1}$, define the stage-$h$ occupancy measure
\[
d_h^\pi(s,a) := \Pr(s_h=s,a_h=a \mid s_0,\pi,P^\star),
\qquad
d_h^\pi(s) := \sum_{a\in\Acal} d_h^\pi(s,a).
\]
Our goal is PAC-RL: given $\epsilon\in(0,1)$ and $\delta\in(0,1)$, output a policy $\hat\pi$ such that
\[
V_0^\star(s_0) - V_0^{\hat\pi}(s_0)\le \epsilon
\qquad\text{with probability at least } 1-\delta.
\]

\paragraph{PAC versus regret.}
This is a \emph{fixed-confidence} guarantee: we aim to return one good policy, rather than control
cumulative suboptimality online (regret).  Regret bounds typically require finer control of how
errors evolve along the interaction; we discuss regret and related work in the bibliographic notes.
As in bandits (Chapter~\ref{chap:bandits}), one can convert PAC guarantees to regret by an
explore--then--commit schedule, yielding sublinear regret rates of the familiar $T^{2/3}$ form.

\paragraph{Hypothesis class and realizability.}
We assume access to a hypothesis class
\[
\Hcal = \Hcal_0 \times \cdots \times \Hcal_{H-1}.
\]
Each hypothesis $f\in\Hcal$ induces functions $\{Q_{h,f}\}_{h=0}^{H-1}$ and $\{V_{h,f}\}_{h=0}^{H-1}$
with the consistency constraint
\[
V_{h,f}(s)=\max_{a\in\Acal} Q_{h,f}(s,a) \qquad \forall (h,s,f),
\qquad V_{H,f}\equiv 0.
\]
Let $\pi_{h,f}(s)\in\arg\max_a Q_{h,f}(s,a)$ and $\pi_f=\{\pi_{h,f}\}_{h=0}^{H-1}$ be the greedy policy
induced by $f$ (ties broken in some fixed manner).

We assume $\Hcal$ is realizable: there exists $f^\star\in\Hcal$ such that $Q_{h,f^\star}=Q_h^\star$
for all $h$ (equivalently, $\pi_{f^\star}$ is optimal).

\section{The Bellman rank}
\label{sec:br_rank_def}

For a fixed stage $h\in\{0,\dots,H-1\}$ and hypothesis $g\in\Hcal$, define the one-step Bellman
residual integrand
\[
\ell_h(s,a,s';g)
\;:=\;
Q_{h,g}(s,a) - r_h(s,a) - V_{h+1,g}(s').
\]

\subsection{$V$-Bellman rank}
\label{subsec:br_v_rank}

Fix hypotheses $f,g\in\Hcal$. The \emph{$V$-average Bellman error} of $g$ under roll-in $d_h^{\pi_f}$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:br_v_bellman_error}
\mathcal{E}_h^{V}(f,g)
:=
\E_{s\sim d_h^{\pi_f},\ a=\pi_{h,g}(s),\ s'\sim P_h^\star(\cdot\mid s,a)}
\big[\ell_h(s,a,s';g)\big].
\end{equation}
Here the state $s$ is drawn from the distribution induced by $\pi_f$, while the action is the greedy
action of $g$ at $s$. Since $V_{h,g}(s)=Q_{h,g}(s,\pi_{h,g}(s))$, $\mathcal{E}_h^{V}(f,g)$ is the expected
$V$-Bellman residual of $g$ on states visited by $\pi_f$.

\begin{definition}[$V$-Bellman rank]
\label{def:v_bellman_rank}
We say $(\Mcal,\Hcal)$ has \emph{$V$-Bellman rank at most $d$} if for each stage $h$ there exist maps
\[
X_h:\Hcal\to\R^d,\qquad W_h:\Hcal\to\R^d
\]
and constants $B_X,B_W\ge 1$ such that
\[
\mathcal{E}_h^{V}(f,g)=\langle X_h(f), W_h(g)\rangle\qquad \forall f,g\in\Hcal,
\]
and $\sup_{f\in\Hcal}\|X_h(f)\|_2\le B_X$, $\sup_{g\in\Hcal}\|W_h(g)\|_2\le B_W$.
\end{definition}

\subsection{$Q$-Bellman rank}
\label{subsec:br_q_rank}

The $Q$-version averages the same residual under the full \emph{state--action} occupancy of $\pi_f$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:br_q_bellman_error}
\mathcal{E}_h^{Q}(f,g)
:=
\E_{(s,a)\sim d_h^{\pi_f},\ s'\sim P_h^\star(\cdot\mid s,a)}
\big[\ell_h(s,a,s';g)\big].
\end{equation}

\begin{definition}[$Q$-Bellman rank]
\label{def:q_bellman_rank}
We say $(\Mcal,\Hcal)$ has \emph{$Q$-Bellman rank at most $d$} if for each stage $h$ there exist maps
\[
X_h:\Hcal\to\R^d,\qquad W_h:\Hcal\to\R^d
\]
and constants $B_X,B_W\ge 1$ such that
\[
\mathcal{E}_h^{Q}(f,g)=\langle X_h(f), W_h(g)\rangle\qquad \forall f,g\in\Hcal,
\]
and $\sup_{f\in\Hcal}\|X_h(f)\|_2\le B_X$, $\sup_{g\in\Hcal}\|W_h(g)\|_2\le B_W$.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Shifting $W_h$ so that $W_h(f^\star)=0$.}
Under realizability, $\mathcal{E}_h^{V}(f,f^\star)=\mathcal{E}_h^{Q}(f,f^\star)=0$ for all $f$ and $h$.
Thus, for either definition, we may assume without loss of generality that $W_h(f^\star)=0$ by replacing
$W_h(g)$ with $W_h(g)-W_h(f^\star)$ (absorbing any constant-factor change into $B_W$).

\paragraph{Why there are two versions.}
The $Q$-version is ``on-policy'' at stage $h$ in the sense that $(s_h,a_h)\sim d_h^{\pi_f}$; consequently,
$\mathcal{E}_h^{Q}(f,g)$ can be estimated directly from data collected by following $\pi_f$.
The $V$-version evaluates the residual at the \emph{action chosen by $g$} on states visited by $\pi_f$.
To estimate $\mathcal{E}_h^{V}(f,g)$ from data that does not depend on $g$, one typically injects
exploration at time $h$ (e.g.\ uniform actions) and uses importance weighting.  This allows data collected
under one roll-in policy to be reused across many candidate $g$, at the cost of explicit $|\Acal|$-dependence
in the estimation error.

\subsection{Two anchor examples}
\label{sec:br_two_examples}

\subsubsection{Contextual linear bandits ($H=1$): small $Q$-Bellman rank}

Let $H=1$ and suppose the initial state is drawn from a distribution $\mu$.
Assume rewards satisfy $\E[r(s,a)\mid s,a]=\langle \theta^\star,\phi(s,a)\rangle$ with
$\phi(s,a)\in\R^d$ and $\|\phi(s,a)\|_2\le 1$.  Take the hypothesis class
\[
\Hcal_0=\{Q_\theta(s,a)=\langle \theta,\phi(s,a)\rangle:\ \|\theta\|_2\le W\},
\]
with $V_\theta(s)=\max_a Q_\theta(s,a)$ and greedy $\pi_\theta$.

\begin{proposition}[Contextual linear bandits have $Q$-Bellman rank at most $d$]
\label{prop:br_ctx_lin_bandit}
The pair $(\Mcal,\Hcal)$ above has $Q$-Bellman rank at most $d$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since $H=1$, $V_{1,g}\equiv 0$ and
\[
\mathcal{E}_0^{Q}(f,g)
=
\E_{s\sim\mu,\ a=\pi_f(s)}\big[Q_g(s,a)-r(s,a)\big]
=
\E_{s\sim\mu,\ a=\pi_f(s)}\langle \theta_g-\theta^\star,\phi(s,a)\rangle.
\]
Define $X_0(f):=\E_{s\sim\mu,\ a=\pi_f(s)}[\phi(s,a)]\in\R^d$ and $W_0(g):=\theta_g-\theta^\star\in\R^d$.
Then $\mathcal{E}_0^{Q}(f,g)=\langle X_0(f),W_0(g)\rangle$.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Linear Bellman completeness: small $Q$-Bellman rank}

We phrase this in the value-based linear Bellman completeness setting (Definition~\ref{def:bc}).
Fix features $\phi:\Scal\times\Acal\to\R^d$ with $\|\phi(s,a)\|_2\le 1$ and take a linear class
\[
\Hcal_h=\Big\{Q_{h,w}(s,a)=\langle w,\phi(s,a)\rangle:\ \|w\|_2\le W,\ \|Q_{h,w}\|_\infty\le H\Big\},
\qquad
V_{h,w}(s)=\max_{a}Q_{h,w}(s,a).
\]
Assume linear Bellman completeness: for each $h$ there exists an operator $\Tcal_h:\R^d\to\R^d$ such that
for all $w\in\R^d$ and all $(s,a)$,
\[
\big(\Tcal_h(w)\big)^\top\phi(s,a)
=
r_h(s,a)
+
\E_{s'\sim P_h^\star(\cdot\mid s,a)}\Big[\max_{a'\in\Acal} w^\top\phi(s',a')\Big],
\]
and $\sup_{h,\|w\|_2\le W}\|\Tcal_h(w)\|_2\le W$.

\begin{proposition}[Linear Bellman completeness implies $Q$-Bellman rank at most $d$]
\label{prop:br_linear_bc}
Under the above assumptions, $(\Mcal,\Hcal)$ has $Q$-Bellman rank at most $d$.
One valid choice is
\[
X_h(f)=\E_{(s_h,a_h)\sim d_h^{\pi_f}}\big[\phi(s_h,a_h)\big],
\qquad
W_h(g)=w_{h,g}-\Tcal_h(w_{h+1,g}).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix $f,g$ and stage $h$. Conditioning on $(s_h,a_h)$ and using the defining identity of $\Tcal_h$,
\begin{align*}
\E\big[\ell_h(s_h,a_h,s_{h+1};g)\mid s_h,a_h\big]
&=
w_{h,g}^\top\phi(s_h,a_h)-r_h(s_h,a_h)-\E\big[V_{h+1,g}(s_{h+1})\mid s_h,a_h\big]\\
&=
w_{h,g}^\top\phi(s_h,a_h)-\big(\Tcal_h(w_{h+1,g})\big)^\top\phi(s_h,a_h)\\
&=
\big(w_{h,g}-\Tcal_h(w_{h+1,g})\big)^\top\phi(s_h,a_h).
\end{align*}
Taking expectation over $(s_h,a_h)\sim d_h^{\pi_f}$ yields
$\mathcal{E}_h^Q(f,g)=\langle X_h(f),W_h(g)\rangle$.
\end{proof}

\section{PAC RL under bounded Bellman rank}
\label{sec:br_pac}

We now give an optimistic-elimination template that works for either the $Q$- or the $V$-version.
The two versions differ only in how we estimate the average Bellman error.

\subsection{Uniform convergence primitive}
\label{subsec:br_epsgen}

For a dataset $\Dcal=\{(s_i,a_i,s_i')\}_{i=1}^m$, we write
\[
\E_{\Dcal}[Z(s,a,s')] := \frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^m Z(s_i,a_i,s_i')
\]
for the empirical average.

\paragraph{Estimating $\mathcal{E}_h^{Q}(f,g)$.}
If $(s,a,s')$ are sampled with $(s,a)\sim d_h^{\pi_f}$ and $s'\sim P_h^\star(\cdot\mid s,a)$, then
$\E_{\Dcal}[\ell_h(s,a,s';g)]$ is an unbiased estimate of $\mathcal{E}_h^Q(f,g)$.

\paragraph{Estimating $\mathcal{E}_h^{V}(f,g)$.}
To estimate $\mathcal{E}_h^V(f,g)$ from data that does not depend on $g$, we use the
importance-weighted loss
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:br_v_loss}
\tilde{\ell}_h^{V}(s,a,s';g)
:=
\frac{\mathbf{1}\{a=\pi_{h,g}(s)\}}{1/A}\,\ell_h(s,a,s';g).
\end{equation}
If $s\sim d_h^{\pi_f}$, $a\sim \mathrm{Unif}(\Acal)$ and $s'\sim P_h^\star(\cdot\mid s,a)$, then
$\E[\tilde{\ell}_h^{V}(s,a,s';g)]=\mathcal{E}_h^{V}(f,g)$.

\begin{assumption}[Uniform convergence of Bellman-error estimates]
\label{assum:br_uniform_conv}
There exists a function $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}(m,\Hcal,\delta)$ such that for any stage $h$,
any distribution $\nu$ over $(s,a,s')$, if $\Dcal=\{(s_i,a_i,s_i')\}_{i=1}^m$ are i.i.d.\ from $\nu$,
then with probability at least $1-\delta$,
\[
\sup_{g\in\Hcal}\left|
\E_{\Dcal}\big[\tilde{\ell}(s,a,s';g)\big]
-
\E_{(s,a,s')\sim\nu}\big[\tilde{\ell}(s,a,s';g)\big]
\right|
\le
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}(m,\Hcal,\delta),
\]
where $\tilde{\ell}$ is either $\ell_h(\cdot;\,g)$ (for the $Q$-version) or
$\tilde{\ell}_h^{V}(\cdot;\,g)$ (for the $V$-version).
\end{assumption}

In Appendix~\ref{subsec:br_epsgen_appendix} we give explicit $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}$ bounds for both
choices of $\tilde{\ell}$.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require iterations $T$, batch size $m$, radius $R$, mode $\mathtt{mode}\in\{\mathtt{Q},\mathtt{V}\}$
\For{$t = 0,1,\dots, T-1$}
    \State Choose
    \[
    f_t \in \argmax_{g\in\Hcal} V_{0,g}(s_0)
    \quad \text{s.t.}\quad
    \sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\Big(\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{i,h}(g)\Big)^2 \le R^2
    \ \ \forall h\in\{0,\dots,H-1\},
    \]
    where $\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{i,h}(g):=\E_{\Dcal_{i,h}}\big[\tilde{\ell}(s,a,s';g)\big]$
    and $\tilde{\ell}$ is chosen according to $\mathtt{mode}$.
    \For{$h=0,1,\dots,H-1$}
        \State Collect a batch $\Dcal_{t,h}=\{(s^j,a^j,(s')^j)\}_{j=1}^m$ by rolling in with $\pi_{f_t}$ to time $h$
        to obtain $s^j\sim d_h^{\pi_{f_t}}$, then
        \[
        a^j=
        \begin{cases}
        \pi_{h,f_t}(s^j), & \mathtt{mode}=\mathtt{Q},\\
        \mathrm{Unif}(\Acal), & \mathtt{mode}=\mathtt{V},
        \end{cases}
        \qquad
        (s')^j\sim P_h^\star(\cdot\mid s^j,a^j).
        \]
    \EndFor
\EndFor
\State Let $\hat t\in\arg\min_{t\in\{0,\dots,T-1\}}\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}\big|\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{t,h}(f_t)\big|$.
\State \textbf{Output:} return $\hat\pi := \pi_{f_{\hat t}}$.
\end{algorithmic}
\caption{Bellman-rank optimistic elimination (Q/V versions)}
\label{alg:br_oe}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Algorithm: Bellman-rank optimistic elimination}
\label{subsec:br_algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:br_oe} maintains a set of hypotheses that are consistent with all previously
collected Bellman-error tests.  At iteration $t$ it selects an \emph{optimistic} hypothesis $f_t$,
maximizing the predicted value $V_{0,g}(s_0)$ subject to the constraint that, at each stage $h$,
the cumulative squared estimated Bellman errors against $g$ over the past batches are small.
It then rolls in with $\pi_{f_t}$ to generate a fresh batch of
transition samples at each stage and repeats. 

The only difference between $\mathtt{mode}=\mathtt{Q}$ and $\mathtt{mode}=\mathtt{V}$ is how we obtain an
unbiased estimate of the average Bellman error at stage $h$.
In the $Q$-version we take the on-policy action $a_h=\pi_{h,f_t}(s_h)$.
In the $V$-version we inject a uniform action at stage $h$ and use the importance-weighted loss
\eqref{eq:br_v_loss}, which permits testing many candidates $g$ using data whose action choices do not
depend on $g$.


\subsection{Main PAC guarantee}
\label{subsec:br_main_thm}

\begin{theorem}[PAC RL under bounded Bellman rank]
\label{thm:br_main}
Fix $\mathtt{mode}\in\{\mathtt{Q},\mathtt{V}\}$.
Assume realizability and that $(\Mcal,\Hcal)$ has Bellman rank at most $d$ in the corresponding sense:
$Q$-Bellman rank if $\mathtt{mode}=\mathtt{Q}$, and $V$-Bellman rank if $\mathtt{mode}=\mathtt{V}$,
with bounds $B_X,B_W$.
Run Algorithm~\ref{alg:br_oe} with batch size $m$ and define
\[
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}} := \varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}\!\left(m,\Hcal,\frac{\delta}{TH}\right).
\]
Set
\[
T := \left\lceil c\,Hd \,\ln\!\Big( eHd\Big(\frac{B_X^2B_W^2}{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}^2}+1\Big)\Big)\right\rceil,
\qquad
R := \sqrt{T}\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}},
\]
for universal constants $c>0$.
Then with probability at least $1-\delta$, the output policy $\hat\pi$ satisfies
\[
V_0^\star(s_0) - V_0^{\hat\pi}(s_0)
\ \le\
C\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}\,
\sqrt{dH^3\ln\!\Big( eHd\Big(\frac{B_X^2B_W^2}{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}^2}+1\Big)\Big)},
\]
for a universal constant $C>0$.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Where the $|\Acal|$ dependence enters.}
The theorem is stated in terms of $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}$.
For $\mathtt{mode}=\mathtt{Q}$, the loss $\tilde{\ell}=\ell_h$ is uniformly $O(H)$-bounded under the
usual range assumptions on $Q_{h,g}$, so standard concentration does not introduce an explicit $|\Acal|$
factor.
For $\mathtt{mode}=\mathtt{V}$, $\tilde{\ell}=\tilde{\ell}_h^V$ includes an importance weight of size $A$,
and $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}$ typically scales linearly with $A$.

\subsection{Instantiations}
\label{subsec:br_instantiations}

Theorem~\ref{thm:br_main} is stated in terms of $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}$.
This section records a few standard plug-ins from Appendix~\ref{subsec:br_epsgen_appendix}.

\begin{corollary}[Finite hypothesis classes]
\label{cor:br_finite_inst}
Assume $|\Hcal|<\infty$ and $\|Q_{h,g}\|_\infty\le H$ for all $h,g$.
Fix $T$ and $m$ and apply Lemma~\ref{lem:br_epsgen_finite} with failure probability $\delta/(TH)$.
Then one may take
\[
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}
\ \le\
3H\sqrt{\frac{2\ln\!\big(2|\Hcal|TH/\delta\big)}{m}}
\qquad\text{for $\mathtt{mode}=\mathtt{Q}$},
\]
and
\[
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}
\ \le\
3AH\sqrt{\frac{2\ln\!\big(2|\Hcal|TH/\delta\big)}{m}}
\qquad\text{for $\mathtt{mode}=\mathtt{V}$}.
\]
Substituting into Theorem~\ref{thm:br_main} yields an explicit PAC guarantee after choosing $m$ so that
the right-hand side is at most $\epsilon$.
\end{corollary}

\paragraph{Beyond finite classes.}
For infinite hypothesis classes, one replaces the finite-class union bound by a standard complexity
measure (covering numbers, VC-type dimensions, Rademacher complexity, etc.).
Appendix~\ref{subsec:br_epsgen_appendix} records one convenient covering-number instantiation for
linear $Q$-classes, which is the one used next. We use this below and
in the next section.

\paragraph{Linear Bellman completeness.}
In the linear Bellman completeness setting (Chapter~\ref{chap:linear_features}), the one-step
Bellman residuals can be written as linear functionals of a $d$-dimensional feature vector.
Proposition~\ref{prop:br_linear_bc} shows this implies bounded $Q$-Bellman rank.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:br_oe} therefore yields PAC guarantees for linear Bellman completeness from
trajectory access alone, complementing the earlier generative-model guarantees for linear Bellman
completeness.

\begin{corollary}[Linear Bellman completeness]
\label{cor:br_linear_bc_inst}
Consider the linear Bellman completeness setting of Proposition~\ref{prop:br_linear_bc}, with feature
dimension $d$ and weight bound $W$ (so $\|w_{h,g}\|_2\le W$ for all $h,g$).
Take $\Hcal$ to be the corresponding product of stage-wise linear classes and run
Algorithm~\ref{alg:br_oe} in $\mathtt{mode}=\mathtt{Q}$.
Then $(\Mcal,\Hcal)$ has $Q$-Bellman rank at most $d$ and Lemma~\ref{lem:br_epsgen_linear} yields
\[
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}
\ \le\
3H\sqrt{\frac{2d\ln(1+4Wm)+\ln\!\big(TH/\delta\big)}{m}}.
\]
In particular, there is a choice of $m$ and $T$ for which Theorem~\ref{thm:br_main} guarantees
$V_0^\star(s_0)-V_0^{\hat\pi}(s_0)\le \epsilon$ with probability at least $1-\delta$, using
\[
m \;=\; \widetilde O\!\left(\frac{d^2 H^5}{\epsilon^2}\right)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
T \;=\; \widetilde O(Hd),
\]
where $\widetilde O(\cdot)$ hides logarithmic factors in $(H,d,W,1/\epsilon,1/\delta)$.
In particular, the sample complexity is polynomial in $(H,d,1/\epsilon)$ and does not depend on $|\Scal|$.
\end{corollary}

As written, Algorithm~\ref{alg:br_oe} collects $m$ independent roll-ins for each stage, for a total of
$O(mHT)$ trajectories of length at most $H$.
In $\mathtt{mode}=\mathtt{Q}$ one can form all batches $\{\Dcal_{t,h}\}_{h=0}^{H-1}$ from the same $m$
full episodes of $\pi_{f_t}$, reducing the interaction cost to $O(mT)$ full episodes.

\section{Model zoo: more bounded Bellman rank examples}
\label{sec:br_model_zoo}

Theorem~\ref{thm:br_main} converts two ingredients into a PAC-RL guarantee: a low-rank factorization
of the average Bellman errors (bounded Bellman rank) and a uniform convergence bound for the
corresponding empirical losses.  In this section we focus on the first ingredient and record
structural assumptions that imply bounded Bellman rank.  To obtain explicit trajectory/sample
complexity statements one then plugs in an appropriate
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}(m,\Hcal,\delta)$ bound from
Appendix~\ref{subsec:br_epsgen_appendix}.  In particular, the appendix includes covering-number
bounds for infinite linear classes and a $\log|\Phi|$ overhead for unions of candidate
representations, which is the basic mechanism behind the feature-selection corollary below.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Linear $Q^\star$ and linear $V^\star$ (a restricted consistent class)}
\label{subsec:br_zoo_linear_qv}

Assume there are known feature maps $\phi:\Scal\times\Acal\to\R^d$ and $\psi:\Scal\to\R^d$ such that
$\sup_{s,a}\|\phi(s,a)\|_2\le 1$ and $\sup_{s}\|\psi(s)\|_2\le 1$, and for each stage $h$,
\[
Q_h^\star(s,a) = \langle \theta_h^\star,\phi(s,a)\rangle,
\qquad
V_h^\star(s) = \langle w_h^\star,\psi(s)\rangle.
\]
Restrict the hypothesis class to pairs $(\theta_h,w_h)$ that are internally consistent:
\[
\Hcal_h
=
\Big\{(\theta,w):\ \|\theta\|_2\le W_1,\ \|w\|_2\le W_2,\ 
\max_{a}\langle\theta,\phi(s,a)\rangle=\langle w,\psi(s)\rangle\ \forall s\Big\}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Linear $Q^\star$ \& $V^\star$ imply bounded $Q$-Bellman rank]
\label{prop:br_linear_qv}
Under the assumptions above, $(\Mcal,\Hcal)$ has $Q$-Bellman rank at most $2d$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix $f,g$ and stage $h$, and write $g$'s parameters as $(\theta_h,w_{h+1})$.
Using $Q_h^\star(s,a)=r_h(s,a)+\E[V_{h+1}^\star(s_{h+1})\mid s_h=s,a_h=a]$,
\begin{align*}
\mathcal{E}_h^{Q}(f,g)
&=
\E_{(s,a)\sim d_h^{\pi_f}}
\Big[\langle \theta_h-\theta_h^\star,\phi(s,a)\rangle
+\Big\langle w_{h+1}-w_{h+1}^\star,\ \E[\psi(s_{h+1})\mid s_h=s,a_h=a]\Big\rangle\Big]\\
&=
\left\langle
\begin{bmatrix}\theta_h-\theta_h^\star\\ w_{h+1}-w_{h+1}^\star\end{bmatrix},
\ \E_{(s,a)\sim d_h^{\pi_f}}
\begin{bmatrix}\phi(s,a)\\ \E[\psi(s_{h+1})\mid s_h=s,a_h=a]\end{bmatrix}
\right\rangle.
\end{align*}
\end{proof}

\paragraph{PAC-RL sample complexity.}
Combining Proposition~\ref{prop:br_linear_qv} with Theorem~\ref{thm:br_main} and the linear-class
uniform convergence bound in Lemma~\ref{lem:br_epsgen_linear} yields a PAC guarantee with sample
complexity polynomial in $(d,H,1/\epsilon)$ (up to logarithmic factors).  We omit the algebra.

\subsection{$Q^\star$-state abstraction}
\label{subsec:br_zoo_abstraction}

Suppose there is an abstraction map $\xi:\Scal\to\Zcal$ with $|\Zcal|\ll|\Scal|$ such that for all $h$,
\[
\xi(s)=\xi(s')\ \Rightarrow\ Q_h^\star(s,a)=Q_h^\star(s',a)\ \forall a,
\quad\text{and}\quad
V_h^\star(s)=V_h^\star(s').
\]
This can be viewed as a special case of Proposition~\ref{prop:br_linear_qv} by taking one-hot features
on $\Zcal$ (and $\Zcal\times\Acal$ for $Q$).  In particular, the relevant complexity parameters depend
on $|\Zcal|$ rather than $|\Scal|$.

\subsection{Low-occupancy structure}
\label{subsec:br_zoo_occupancy}

\begin{proposition}[Low state-action occupancy implies bounded $Q$-Bellman rank]
\label{prop:br_low_occ_q}
Assume that for each stage $h$ there exist maps $\beta_h:\Hcal\to\R^d$ and
$\psi_h:\Scal\times\Acal\to\R^d$ such that for all $f\in\Hcal$,
$d_h^{\pi_f}(s,a)=\langle \beta_h(f),\psi_h(s,a)\rangle$.
Then any realizable $\Hcal$ has $Q$-Bellman rank at most $d$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For any $f,g$,
\[
\mathcal{E}_h^{Q}(f,g)
=
\sum_{s,a} d_h^{\pi_f}(s,a)\,\E[\ell_h(s,a,s';g)\mid s,a]
=
\Big\langle \beta_h(f),\ \sum_{s,a}\psi_h(s,a)\,\E[\ell_h(s,a,s';g)\mid s,a]\Big\rangle.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Low state occupancy implies bounded $V$-Bellman rank]
\label{prop:br_low_occ_v}
Assume that for each stage $h$ there exist maps $\beta_h:\Hcal\to\R^d$ and $\psi_h:\Scal\to\R^d$
such that for all $f\in\Hcal$, $d_h^{\pi_f}(s)=\langle \beta_h(f),\psi_h(s)\rangle$.
Then any realizable $\Hcal$ has $V$-Bellman rank at most $d$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For any $f,g$,
\[
\mathcal{E}_h^{V}(f,g)
=
\sum_{s} d_h^{\pi_f}(s)\,\E[\ell_h(s,\pi_{h,g}(s),s';g)\mid s]
=
\Big\langle \beta_h(f),\ \sum_{s}\psi_h(s)\,\E[\ell_h(s,\pi_{h,g}(s),s';g)\mid s]\Big\rangle.
\]
\end{proof}

\paragraph{PAC-RL sample complexity.}
If the family of roll-in occupancies $\{d_h^{\pi_f}: f\in\Hcal\}$ lies in a low-dimensional linear
span (in either the state-action or state sense), then Theorem~\ref{thm:br_main} yields PAC guarantees
with complexity governed by that dimension.  The statistical term is obtained by selecting an
appropriate $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}$ bound for the hypothesis class $\Hcal$
(Appendix~\ref{subsec:br_epsgen_appendix}).

\subsection{Low-rank transitions and representation learning}
\label{subsec:br_zoo_lowrank_rep}

Assume a low-rank transition factorization: there exist unknown maps
$\mu_h^\star:\Scal\to\R^d$ and $\phi_h^\star:\Scal\times\Acal\to\R^d$ such that for all $h$,
\[
P_h^\star(s'\mid s,a)=\langle \mu_h^\star(s'),\phi_h^\star(s,a)\rangle,
\qquad
\sup_{s,a}\|\phi_h^\star(s,a)\|_2\le 1.
\]
We also assume a mild normalization: there exists $B_\mu\ge 1$ such that for all bounded
$f:\Scal\to\R$ and all $h$,
\[
\bigg\|\sum_{s\in\Scal}\mu_h^\star(s)\,f(s)\bigg\|_2 \le B_\mu\,\|f\|_\infty.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Low-rank transitions imply bounded $V$-Bellman rank]
\label{prop:br_lowrank_transitions}
Assume the low-rank transition factorization above and that $\|Q_{h,g}\|_\infty\le H$ for all $h,g\in\Hcal$
(and hence $\|V_{h,g}\|_\infty\le H$).
Then $(\Mcal,\Hcal)$ has $V$-Bellman rank at most $d$.
One valid choice satisfies $\sup_f\|X_h(f)\|_2\le 1$ and $\sup_g\|W_h(g)\|_2\le B_\mu H$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix a stage $h$ and hypotheses $f,g$.

For $h=0$, the roll-in state is always $s_0$, so $\mathcal{E}_0^{V}(f,g)$ depends only on $g$.
Let $u\in\R^d$ be any fixed unit vector. Define $X_0(\cdot)\equiv u$ and
$W_0(g):=\mathcal{E}_0^{V}(\cdot,g)\,u$. Then
$\mathcal{E}_0^{V}(f,g)=\langle X_0(f),W_0(g)\rangle$ and $\|W_0(g)\|_2\le H$.

Now assume $h\ge 1$. Define
\[
X_h(f)
\;:=\;
\E_{(s,a)\sim d_{h-1}^{\pi_f}}\!\big[\phi_{h-1}^\star(s,a)\big]\in\R^d.
\]
By $\|\phi_{h-1}^\star\|_2\le 1$, we have $\|X_h(f)\|_2\le 1$.
Let $p_h^{\pi_f}$ denote the stage-$h$ state distribution under $\pi_f$.
Using the low-rank factorization and the definition of occupancy,
\begin{align*}
p_h^{\pi_f}(s)
&=
\sum_{s',a'} d_{h-1}^{\pi_f}(s',a')\,P_{h-1}^\star(s\mid s',a')\\
&=
\sum_{s',a'} d_{h-1}^{\pi_f}(s',a')\,\langle \mu_{h-1}^\star(s),\phi_{h-1}^\star(s',a')\rangle\\
&=
\Big\langle \mu_{h-1}^\star(s),\ \E_{(s',a')\sim d_{h-1}^{\pi_f}}\big[\phi_{h-1}^\star(s',a')\big]\Big\rangle
=
\langle \mu_{h-1}^\star(s), X_h(f)\rangle.
\end{align*}

Next define the state-wise expected residual under $g$ at stage $h$:
\[
b_h(s;g)
\;:=\;
\E_{a=\pi_{h,g}(s),\ s'\sim P_h^\star(\cdot\mid s,a)}\big[\ell_h(s,a,s';g)\big].
\]
Since $\ell_h(\cdot;\,g)$ is bounded in $[-H,H]$ under the range assumptions on $Q$ and $V$,
we have $\|b_h(\cdot;g)\|_\infty\le H$.
Using the definition of $\mathcal{E}_h^V$ and the identity for $p_h^{\pi_f}$,
\begin{align*}
\mathcal{E}_h^{V}(f,g)
&=
\sum_{s\in\Scal} p_h^{\pi_f}(s)\,b_h(s;g)
=
\sum_{s\in\Scal}\langle \mu_{h-1}^\star(s), X_h(f)\rangle\,b_h(s;g)\\
&=
\Big\langle X_h(f),\ \sum_{s\in\Scal}\mu_{h-1}^\star(s)\,b_h(s;g)\Big\rangle.
\end{align*}
Define
\[
W_h(g)
\;:=\;
\sum_{s\in\Scal}\mu_{h-1}^\star(s)\,b_h(s;g)\in\R^d.
\]
Then $\mathcal{E}_h^{V}(f,g)=\langle X_h(f),W_h(g)\rangle$.
Moreover, by the normalization assumption and $\|b_h(\cdot;g)\|_\infty\le H$,
$\|W_h(g)\|_2\le B_\mu H$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Why this suggests representation learning.}
Proposition~\ref{prop:br_lowrank_transitions} shows that bounded Bellman rank can arise from a
\emph{latent} low-dimensional transition structure, even when the feature map
$\phi_h^\star$ is not known.  If the learner searches over a family $\Phi$ of candidate
representations, the Bellman-rank parameter can remain $d$ while the statistical error pays only a
$\log|\Phi|$ overhead via uniform convergence (Lemma~\ref{lem:br_epsgen_union_linear}), which is the
basic mechanism behind sparse feature selection.

\subsection{Sparse linear MDPs and feature selection}
\label{subsec:br_zoo_sparse}

A sparse linear MDP is a special case of the low-rank model in which the ambient feature map
$\phi:\Scal\times\Acal\to\R^D$ is known but only an unknown subset of coordinates matters.
Let $K^\star\subset[D]$ be an unknown support with $|K^\star|=s\ll D$, and write $\phi_K(s,a)\in\R^s$
for the restriction to coordinates in $K$.
Assume the true transitions admit a rank-$s$ factorization in these coordinates (so the latent
dimension is $s$).  Then Proposition~\ref{prop:br_lowrank_transitions} implies bounded $V$-Bellman rank
with parameter $d=s$ (up to the normalization constant $B_\mu$).

\paragraph{PAC-RL sample complexity.}
If $K^\star$ is unknown, a natural strategy is to take a union over candidate supports $K$.
The Bellman-rank parameter is governed by the \emph{latent} dimension $s$, while the statistical term
depends on the candidate family only through $\log|\Phi|$ in the union bound
(Appendix~\ref{subsec:br_epsgen_appendix}).

\begin{corollary}[Feature selection: polynomial in $s$, polylogarithmic in $D$]
\label{cor:br_feature_select_inst}
Fix $s\in\{1,\dots,D\}$ and a finite family $\Phi$ of candidate supports $K\subset[D]$ with $|K|=s$
(e.g.\ all $s$-subsets).
For each $K\in\Phi$, let $\Hcal(\phi_K)$ be a stage-wise linear $Q$-class as in the appendix, and take
the union hypothesis class $\Hcal:=\bigcup_{K\in\Phi}\Hcal(\phi_K)$.

Assume realizability and that $(\Mcal,\Hcal)$ has $V$-Bellman rank at most $s$ (with some bounds
$B_X,B_W$), and run Algorithm~\ref{alg:br_oe} in $\mathtt{mode}=\mathtt{V}$.
Then $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}$ depends on $\Phi$ only through $\ln|\Phi|$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:br_epsgen_union_linear}).
In particular, when $\Phi$ is the family of all $s$-subsets of $[D]$, $\ln|\Phi|\le s\ln(eD/s)$, so the
resulting PAC sample complexity is polynomial in $s$ and polylogarithmic in $D$ (up to additional
logarithmic factors in $(H,A,1/\epsilon,1/\delta)$).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:br_epsgen_union_linear} bounds $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}(m,\Hcal,\delta)$ for the union
class $\Hcal$ with $\ln(1/\delta)$ replaced by $\ln(|\Phi|/\delta)$.
Substituting this $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}$ into Theorem~\ref{thm:br_main} yields a PAC bound whose
dependence on the candidate family enters only through $\ln|\Phi|$.  For $\Phi=\{K\subset[D]:|K|=s\}$,
we use $\ln|\Phi|=\ln\binom{D}{s}\le s\ln(eD/s)$.
\end{proof}

\section{Analysis}
\label{subsec:br_analysis}

We prove Theorem~\ref{thm:br_main}. Fix $\mathtt{mode}\in\{\mathtt{Q},\mathtt{V}\}$.
For each stage $h$, let $\tilde{\ell}_h(\cdot;\,g)$ denote the loss used in the constraints and in
data collection: $\tilde{\ell}_h=\ell_h$ for $\mathtt{mode}=\mathtt{Q}$, and
$\tilde{\ell}_h=\tilde{\ell}_h^V$ for $\mathtt{mode}=\mathtt{V}$.

For each iteration $t$ and stage $h$, the algorithm collects an i.i.d.\ batch
$\Dcal_{t,h}=\{(s_i,a_i,s_i')\}_{i=1}^m$ by rolling in with $\pi_{f_t}$ to time $h$ and then choosing
actions according to the $\mathtt{mode}$-dependent rule at time $h$.
Define the empirical Bellman-error estimate
\[
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{t,h}(g)
\;:=\;
\E_{\Dcal_{t,h}}\big[\tilde{\ell}_h(s,a,s';g)\big]
\;=\;
\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^m \tilde{\ell}_h(s_i,a_i,s_i';g).
\]
By construction (and by the importance-weighting identity in the $V$-case),
\[
\E\big[\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{t,h}(g)\mid \mathcal{H}_{<t}\big]
\;=\;
\mathcal{E}^{\mathtt{mode}}_h(f_t,g),
\]
where $\mathcal{E}^{\mathtt{mode}}_h$ denotes $\mathcal{E}^Q_h$ if $\mathtt{mode}=\mathtt{Q}$ and
$\mathcal{E}^V_h$ if $\mathtt{mode}=\mathtt{V}$.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform estimation event]
\label{lem:br_good_event}
Let $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}:=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}(m,\Hcal,\delta/(TH))$.
With probability at least $1-\delta$, simultaneously for all $t\in\{0,\dots,T-1\}$, all
$h\in\{0,\dots,H-1\}$, and all $g\in\Hcal$,
\[
\big|\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{t,h}(g)-\mathcal{E}^{\mathtt{mode}}_h(f_t,g)\big|
\ \le\ 
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Condition on $\mathcal{H}_{<t}$. Then $\Dcal_{t,h}$ is i.i.d.\ from some distribution $\nu_{t,h}$
over $(s,a,s')$. Assumption~\ref{assum:br_uniform_conv} applied with failure probability $\delta/(TH)$
gives the desired bound for this fixed pair $(t,h)$. Union bound over $t$ and $h$ completes the proof.
\end{proof}

In the rest of the proof we condition on the event of Lemma~\ref{lem:br_good_event}.
We also apply the standard shift so that $W_h(f^\star)=0$ for all $h$, absorbing any constant-factor
change into $B_W$.

\begin{lemma}[Feasibility and optimism]
\label{lem:br_feasible_optimistic}
On the event of Lemma~\ref{lem:br_good_event}, if $R=\sqrt{T}\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}$ then
$f^\star$ is feasible in every iteration $t$, and hence $V_{0,f_t}(s_0)\ge V_0^\star(s_0)$ for all $t$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $t$ and $h$. Realizability implies $Q_{h,f^\star}=Q_h^\star$ and $V_{h+1,f^\star}=V_{h+1}^\star$.
Therefore, for any $(s,a)$,
\[
\E_{s'\sim P_h^\star(\cdot\mid s,a)}\big[\ell_h(s,a,s';f^\star)\big]
=
Q_h^\star(s,a)-r_h(s,a)-\E[V_{h+1}^\star(s')]
=0.
\]
It follows that $\mathcal{E}_h^{\mathtt{mode}}(f,f^\star)=0$ for every roll-in $f$ (both
$\mathtt{Q}$ and $\mathtt{V}$). By Lemma~\ref{lem:br_good_event},
$|\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{i,h}(f^\star)|\le \varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}$ for all $i\in\{0,\dots,t-1\}$.
Hence
\[
\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\big(\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{i,h}(f^\star)\big)^2
\le
t\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}^2
\le
T\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}^2
=R^2,
\]
so $f^\star$ is feasible at iteration $t$. Since $f_t$ maximizes $V_{0,g}(s_0)$ over the feasible set and
$V_{0,f^\star}(s_0)=V_0^\star(s_0)$, we have $V_{0,f_t}(s_0)\ge V_0^\star(s_0)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Performance bound via self Bellman error]
\label{lem:br_perf_decomp}
For every $t$,
\[
V_0^\star(s_0)-V_0^{\pi_{f_t}}(s_0)
\ \le\
\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}\Big|\mathcal{E}^{\mathtt{mode}}_h(f_t,f_t)\Big|
\ =\
\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}\Big|\langle X_h(f_t),W_h(f_t)\rangle\Big|.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:br_feasible_optimistic}, $V_0^\star(s_0)\le V_{0,f_t}(s_0)$, so
\[
V_0^\star(s_0)-V_0^{\pi_{f_t}}(s_0)\le V_{0,f_t}(s_0)-V_0^{\pi_{f_t}}(s_0).
\]
Let $(s_h,a_h)_{h=0}^{H-1}$ be the trajectory generated by executing $\pi_{f_t}$ in the true MDP.
Since $a_h=\pi_{h,f_t}(s_h)$ and $V_{h,f_t}(s_h)=Q_{h,f_t}(s_h,a_h)$,
\[
V_{0,f_t}(s_0)-\sum_{h=0}^{H-1} r_h(s_h,a_h)
=
\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}\big(Q_{h,f_t}(s_h,a_h)-r_h(s_h,a_h)-V_{h+1,f_t}(s_{h+1})\big).
\]
Taking expectation over the trajectory gives
\[
V_{0,f_t}(s_0)-V_0^{\pi_{f_t}}(s_0)
=
\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}\E\big[\ell_h(s_h,a_h,s_{h+1};f_t)\big]
=
\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}\mathcal{E}^{\mathtt{mode}}_h(f_t,f_t),
\]
and the absolute-value bound follows. Finally, Bellman rank gives
$\mathcal{E}^{\mathtt{mode}}_h(f_t,f_t)=\langle X_h(f_t),W_h(f_t)\rangle$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:br_main}]
Fix a regularization parameter $\lambda>0$ (chosen at the end) and define, for each stage $h$,
\[
\Sigma_{t,h}:=\lambda I+\sum_{i=0}^{t-1} X_h(f_i)X_h(f_i)^\top,
\qquad
\Sigma_{0,h}=\lambda I .
\]

First, we put bound on $\|W_h(f_t)\|_{\Sigma_{t,h}}$.
Since $f_t$ is feasible,
$\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}(\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{i,h}(f_t))^2\le R^2$.
On the good event, for each $i\in\{0,\dots,t-1\}$,
\[
\big|\langle X_h(f_i),W_h(f_t)\rangle\big|
=
\big|\mathcal{E}^{\mathtt{mode}}_h(f_i,f_t)\big|
\le
\big|\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{i,h}(f_t)\big|+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}.
\]
Thus,
\[
\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\langle X_h(f_i),W_h(f_t)\rangle^2
\le
2\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\big(\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{i,h}(f_t)\big)^2 + 2t\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}^2
\le
2R^2+2T\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}^2
=
4T\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}^2.
\]
Therefore,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:br_W_norm_bound}
\|W_h(f_t)\|_{\Sigma_{t,h}}^2
=
\lambda\|W_h(f_t)\|_2^2 + \sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\langle X_h(f_i),W_h(f_t)\rangle^2
\le
\lambda B_W^2 + 4T\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}^2.
\end{equation}

Now, we bound $\|X_h(f_t)\|_{\Sigma_{t,h}^{-1}}$ for a given $t$.
For each fixed $h$, the matrix determinant lemma gives
\[
\det(\Sigma_{t+1,h})=\det(\Sigma_{t,h})\Big(1+\|X_h(f_t)\|_{\Sigma_{t,h}^{-1}}^2\Big),
\]
so summing over $t=0,\dots,T-1$ yields
\[
\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\ln\Big(1+\|X_h(f_t)\|_{\Sigma_{t,h}^{-1}}^2\Big)
=
\ln\frac{\det(\Sigma_{T,h})}{\det(\lambda I)}.
\]
Since $\|X_h(f_t)\|_2\le B_X$,
\[
\mathrm{tr}(\Sigma_{T,h})
\le d\lambda + \sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\|X_h(f_t)\|_2^2
\le d\lambda + T B_X^2.
\]
Using $\det(M)\le (\mathrm{tr}(M)/d)^d$ for PSD $M$ gives
\[
\ln\frac{\det(\Sigma_{T,h})}{\det(\lambda I)}
\le
d\ln\Big(1+\frac{T B_X^2}{d\lambda}\Big).
\]
Summing over $h$ and averaging over $t$ yields
\[
\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}\ln\Big(1+\|X_h(f_t)\|_{\Sigma_{t,h}^{-1}}^2\Big)
\le
\frac{Hd}{T}\ln\Big(1+\frac{T B_X^2}{d\lambda}\Big).
\]
Hence there exists $t^\star\in\{0,\dots,T-1\}$ such that
\[
\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}\ln\Big(1+\|X_h(f_{t^\star})\|_{\Sigma_{t^\star,h}^{-1}}^2\Big)
\le
\frac{Hd}{T}\ln\Big(1+\frac{T B_X^2}{d\lambda}\Big).
\]
Since each summand is nonnegative, this implies that for every $h$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:br_X_norm_bound}
\|X_h(f_{t^\star})\|_{\Sigma_{t^\star,h}^{-1}}^2
\le
\exp\!\left(\frac{Hd}{T}\ln\Big(1+\frac{T B_X^2}{d\lambda}\Big)\right)-1.
\end{equation}


By Lemma~\ref{lem:br_perf_decomp} and Cauchy--Schwarz,
\begin{align*}
V_0^\star(s_0)-V_0^{\pi_{f_{t^\star}}}(s_0)
&\le
\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}\|X_h(f_{t^\star})\|_{\Sigma_{t^\star,h}^{-1}}\,
\|W_h(f_{t^\star})\|_{\Sigma_{t^\star,h}} \\
&\le
H\sqrt{\lambda B_W^2 + 4T\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}^2}\;
\sqrt{\exp\!\left(\frac{Hd}{T}\ln\Big(1+\frac{T B_X^2}{d\lambda}\Big)\right)-1},
\end{align*}
using \eqref{eq:br_W_norm_bound} and \eqref{eq:br_X_norm_bound}.

Now choose
\[
\lambda := \frac{B_X^2}{\frac{B_X^2B_W^2}{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}^2}+1},
\qquad
L := \ln\!\Big(eHd\Big(\frac{B_X^2B_W^2}{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}^2}+1\Big)\Big),
\qquad
T := \left\lceil c\,Hd\,L\right\rceil,
\]
with $c>0$ a sufficiently large universal constant.
Then $\lambda B_W^2\le \varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}^2$ and, since $T\asymp Hd\,L$,
\[
\ln\Big(1+\frac{T B_X^2}{d\lambda}\Big)
=
\ln\Big(1+\frac{T}{d}\Big(\frac{B_X^2B_W^2}{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}^2}+1\Big)\Big)
\le
L + \ln(3cL),
\]
which implies (for $c$ large enough) that
\[
\frac{Hd}{T}\ln\Big(1+\frac{T B_X^2}{d\lambda}\Big)\le 1.
\]
Using $\exp(x)-1\le 2x$ for $x\in[0,1]$ yields
\[
V_0^\star(s_0)-V_0^{\pi_{f_{t^\star}}}(s_0)
\ \le\
C_0\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}\sqrt{dH^3\,L}
\]
for a universal constant $C_0>0$.

Finally, we select the output iterate.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:br_oe} returns $\hat\pi=\pi_{f_{\hat t}}$ where
$\hat t\in\arg\min_{t\in\{0,\dots,T-1\}}\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}\big|\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{t,h}(f_t)\big|$.
On the good event, for every $t$ and $h$,
\[
\big|\mathcal{E}_h^{\mathtt{mode}}(f_t,f_t)\big|
\le
\big|\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{t,h}(f_t)\big|+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}},
\qquad
\big|\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{t,h}(f_t)\big|
\le
\big|\mathcal{E}_h^{\mathtt{mode}}(f_t,f_t)\big|+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}.
\]
Therefore,
\begin{align*}
\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}\big|\mathcal{E}_h^{\mathtt{mode}}(f_{\hat t},f_{\hat t})\big|
&\le
\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}\big|\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{\hat t,h}(f_{\hat t})\big| + H\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}} \\
&\le
\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}\big|\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{t^\star,h}(f_{t^\star})\big| + H\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}} \\
&\le
\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}\big|\mathcal{E}_h^{\mathtt{mode}}(f_{t^\star},f_{t^\star})\big| + 2H\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}.
\end{align*}
Combining with Lemma~\ref{lem:br_perf_decomp} and the bound for $t^\star$ gives
\[
V_0^\star(s_0)-V_0^{\hat\pi}(s_0)
\le
C_0\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}\sqrt{dH^3\,L} + 2H\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}.
\]
Since $L=\ln(eHd(\cdots))\ge 1$ and $d\ge 1$, the additive term $2H\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gen}}$ is
dominated by the leading term up to a constant factor, yielding the stated bound of
Theorem~\ref{thm:br_main}.
\end{proof}


\section{Bibliographic Remarks and Further Readings}\label{ch7:bib}

The Bellman rank was introduced by \citet{jiang2017contextual} as a structural condition
under which data collected under one policy can be reused to test many candidate value functions.
The original formulation corresponds to the \emph{$V$-version} used in this chapter.
Subsequent work clarified that there are two natural variants, depending on whether the
average Bellman error is defined under the state--action occupancy of the roll-in policy
(the \emph{$Q$-version}) or under roll-in states paired with the greedy action of the tested
hypothesis (the \emph{$V$-version}); see \citet{bilinear,jin2021bellman}.
We focus on PAC-RL guarantees (returning one near-optimal policy). Regret guarantees are
subtler, particularly for the $V$-version, because estimating $\mathcal{E}_h^V(f,g)$ typically
requires explicit exploration or importance weighting rather than a purely on-policy estimate.
In the regret setting, \citet{dong2020root} obtained $\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{T})$ regret under a
$V$-Bellman-rank-type assumption using a careful sampling and elimination procedure.
For the $Q$-version, on-policy estimation enables more direct optimistic algorithms:
\citet{jin2021bellman} established $\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{T})$ regret guarantees in this framework.
Related regret bounds under linear Bellman completeness were obtained earlier by
\citet{zanette2020learning}.

A closely related perspective is the \emph{bilinear class} framework of \citet{bilinear}.
The bilinear class can be viewed as a natural extension of Bellman rank that unifies a
number of low-complexity models through low-rank factorizations of average Bellman errors.
While we do not define the full bilinear class in this chapter, our definitions of $Q$- and
$V$-Bellman rank follow the variants emphasized in \citet{bilinear,jin2021bellman}, and many of the
examples here are drawn from \citet{jiang2017contextual} and \citet{bilinear}.
The analysis in \citet{bilinear} also motivates the elliptical-potential style argument used in our
PAC proof.

Another complexity measure is the \emph{Eluder dimension}, introduced by \citet{eluder} for
bandits and extended to reinforcement learning in, e.g.,
\citet{osband2014model,wang2020reinforcement,ayoub2020model,jin2021bellman}.
The Bellman--Eluder dimension and bilinear-rank-type conditions are distinct.
At a high level, bilinear rank is often straightforward to verify for standard parametric models,
and it captures many settings where RL is known to be statistically efficient.
The Bellman--Eluder dimension can be viewed as a different way to control exploration by quantifying
how quickly the learner can rule out hypotheses.  We refer the reader to \citet{jin2021bellman} for a
detailed treatment and comparisons.  The bilinear class also subsumes witness-rank-type conditions
\citep{sun2019model}, yielding coverage of models such as factored
MDPs \citep{kearns1999efficient};  see \citet{bilinear} for a
discussion of separations where Bellman rank or Bellman--Eluder
dimension can be exponentially larger than bilinear-rank-based measures. 

We conclude with pointers on several special cases highlighted in this chapter.
Feature selection and representation learning in low-rank models appear already in
\citet{jiang2017contextual}.  Later work developed oracle-efficient approaches and refined statistical
guarantees; see \citet{agarwal2020flambe,uehara2021representation}.
Model-free approaches were also studied, for example by
\citet{modi2021model}.  Sparse linear MDP models were proposed in \citet{hao2021online} (see also related work on feature selection and sparsity in structured MDPs).
The linear mixture model was introduced and analyzed in
\citet{modi2020sample,ayoub2020model,zhou2021provably,zhou2021nearly}.
The linear $Q^\star$--$V^\star$ model and the low-occupancy model
appear explicitly in \citet{bilinear}. 

Finally, \citet{foster2023dec} studies an asymptotic minimax view of interactive learning and proposes
the \emph{decision--estimation coefficient} (DEC), a complexity measure characterized via a Le Cam
notion of statistical estimation and shown to be both necessary and sufficient (in that asymptotic
sense) for sample-efficient interactive learning.
