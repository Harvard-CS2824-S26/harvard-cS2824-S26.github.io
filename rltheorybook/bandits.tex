\chapter{Multi-Armed \& Linear Bandits}
\label{chap:bandits}

When $\gamma=0$ (equivalently $H=1$ in the finite-horizon setting), learning in an
unknown MDP reduces to the multi-armed bandit problem. The basic algorithms and
proof techniques in this chapter are important in their own right, and we will
later extend these ideas to handle exploration--exploitation tradeoffs in more
general reinforcement learning problems.

Throughout this chapter, rewards are stochastic. In the case of linear
bandits, we will be interested in a setting where the number of
possible decisions is large (or uncountably infinite).

\section{The $K$-Armed Bandit Problem}

There are $K$ actions (``arms''). Pulling arm $a\in[K]$ produces a random reward
$r_a\in[-1,1]$ distributed according to an unknown distribution
$R(a)\in\Delta([-1,1])$, with mean
\[
\mu_a := \E_{r_a\sim R(a)}[r_a]\in[-1,1].
\]
The interaction proceeds for $T$ rounds, indexed by $t=0,1,\dots,T-1$. At round
$t$, the learner selects an arm $I_t\in[K]$ and observes a reward
\[
r_t \sim R(I_t),\qquad \E[r_t\mid I_t=a]=\mu_a.
\]

\paragraph{Regret.}
The (cumulative) regret compares our total reward to that of the best fixed arm:
\[
R_T \;:=\; T\mu_\star - \sum_{t=0}^{T-1} r_t,
\qquad \text{where}\quad
\mu_\star := \max_{a\in[K]}\mu_a.
\]
For the analysis it is convenient to work with the (pseudo) regret
\[
R_T \;:=\; T\mu_\star - \sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mu_{I_t},
\]
and we will refer to this quantity as the regret throughout.
(One can relate $\sum_{t=0}^{T-1} r_t$ to $\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mu_{I_t}$ via
Hoeffding--Azuma, since the reward noise forms a martingale difference sequence.)

Let $a^\star\in\argmax_{a\in[K]}\mu_a$ be an optimal arm and define the gap
\[
\Delta_a := \mu_{a^\star}-\mu_a \ge 0.
\]


\subsection{A baseline: explore--then--commit}

A natural first idea is to \emph{explore} uniformly to estimate the arm means,
then \emph{commit} to the empirically best arm.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require exploration length $m\in\mathbb{N}$
\State Pull each arm $a\in[K]$ exactly $m$ times (in any order), and compute the empirical mean $\widehat\mu(a)$ from these $m$ samples.
\State Let $\widehat a \in \argmax_{a\in[K]}\widehat\mu(a)$.
\State For all remaining rounds, play $I_t=\widehat a$.
\end{algorithmic}
\caption{Explore--Then--Commit (ETC)}
\label{alg:etc}
\end{algorithm}

ETC is easy to analyze and motivates why \emph{adaptive} exploration (e.g., UCB)
is needed: if we knew the gaps, we could choose $m$ so that the best arm is
identified with high probability, giving gap-dependent regret. Without gap
knowledge, fixed exploration can be suboptimal in the worst case.

\begin{proposition}[ETC, gap-dependent bound]\label{prop:etc_gap}
Fix $\delta\in(0,1)$. Suppose ETC explores each arm $m$ times, and then commits.
If
\[
m \;\ge\; \frac{2}{\Delta_{\min}^2}\,\ln\!\Big(\frac{2K}{\delta}\Big),
\qquad\text{where}\quad
\Delta_{\min}:=\min_{a\neq a^\star}\Delta_a,
\]
then with probability at least $1-\delta$, ETC selects $\widehat a=a^\star$ and
\[
R_T \;\le\; m\sum_{a\neq a^\star}\Delta_a
\;=\;
\frac{2 \sum_{a\neq a^\star}\Delta_a}{\Delta_{\min}^2}\,\ln\!\Big(\frac{2K}{\delta}\Big).
\]
\end{proposition}

\noindent
\emph{Proof sketch.} After $m$ pulls of each arm, Hoeffding's inequality
(Lemma~\ref{lem:hoeffding}) implies that simultaneously for all $a\in[K]$,
$|\widehat\mu(a)-\mu_a|\le \sqrt{2\ln(2K/\delta)/m}$ with probability $\ge 1-\delta$.
If this deviation is at most $\Delta_{\min}/2$, then $\widehat\mu(a^\star)>\widehat\mu(a)$
for all $a\neq a^\star$, so the algorithm commits to $a^\star$ and regret comes only
from the exploration pulls. \qed

\medskip

We now turn to an adaptive method (UCB) which automatically balances exploration
and exploitation and achieves both a distribution-free $\tilde O(\sqrt{KT})$
regret bound and a gap-dependent $O(\sum_{a\neq a^\star}\tfrac{\log T}{\Delta_a})$
bound. Note that, for the gap dependent case, this bound improves upon
ECT by, roughly, a $1/\Delta_{\min}$ factor.

\subsection{The Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) Algorithm}

We now present and analyze the upper confidence bound (UCB) algorithm (Algorithm~\ref{alg:ucb}).
For simplicity, we ensure every arm has been pulled at
least once by allocating the first $K$ rounds to pulling each arm once.
Here $N_t(a)$ is the number of times arm $a$ was pulled prior to round $t$:
\[
N_t(a) := \sum_{s=0}^{t-1}\mathbf{1}\{I_s=a\},
\]
and the empirical mean is
\[
\widehat\mu_t(a) := \frac{1}{N_t(a)}\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}\mathbf{1}\{I_s=a\}\,r_s.
\]
(After the initialization phase, $N_t(a)\ge 1$ for all $a$ and $t\ge K$.)

The algorithm enjoys the following guarantee:

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:mab_main}
With probability at least $1-\delta$, the UCB algorithm obtains the
following regret guarantee:
\[
R_T
= O\!\left(
\min\left\{
\sqrt{KT\,\ln\!\Big(\frac{TK}{\delta}\Big)},
\;\;
\sum_{a\neq a^\star}\frac{\ln\!\big(\frac{TK}{\delta}\big)}{\Delta_a}
\right\}
+ K
\right).
\]
\end{theorem}


\begin{algorithm}[t]
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require horizon $T$, failure probability $\delta$
\For{$a=1,2,\dots,K$}
    \State Play arm $I_{a-1}=a$ and observe reward $r_{a-1}$.
\EndFor
\For{$t=K,K+1,\dots,T-1$}
    \State Set $I_t \in \argmax_{a\in[K]}\left(\widehat\mu_t(a) + \sqrt{\frac{2\ln(2TK/\delta)}{N_t(a)}}\right)$.
    \State Play arm $I_t$ and observe reward $r_t$.
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\caption{UCB}
\label{alg:ucb}
\end{algorithm}


We define the UCB index
\[
\mathrm{UCB}_t(a)
:= \widehat\mu_t(a) + \sqrt{\frac{2\ln(2TK/\delta)}{N_t(a)}}.
\]
The following lemma shows that, uniformly over all arms and all times, this is a
valid confidence bound with high probability.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform concentration of empirical means]\label{lem:mab_ucb_event}
With probability at least $1-\delta$, simultaneously for all rounds $t\in\{K,\dots,T-1\}$
and all arms $a\in[K]$,
\[
\big|\widehat\mu_t(a)-\mu_a\big|
\;\le\;
\sqrt{\frac{2\ln(2TK/\delta)}{N_t(a)}}.
\]
\end{lemma}


\begin{proof} For each arm $a$ and each pull-count $n\in\{1,\dots,T\}$, let
$\widehat\mu_{a,n}$ denote the empirical mean of the first $n$ rewards obtained from arm $a$.
Hoeffding's inequality gives
$\Pr(|\widehat\mu_{a,n}-\mu_a|>\sqrt{2\ln(2TK/\delta)/n})\le \delta/(TK)$.
A union bound over all $(a,n)\in[K]\times[T]$ yields the claim, and at time $t$
we simply take $n=N_t(a)$. 
\end{proof}

Now provide the proof of UCB.

\begin{proof} (Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:mab_main}.)
Let
\[
\mathrm{rad}_t(a) \;:=\; \sqrt{\frac{2\ln(2TK/\delta)}{N_t(a)}},
\qquad
\mathrm{UCB}_t(a) \;:=\; \widehat\mu_t(a) + \mathrm{rad}_t(a).
\]
Define the high-probability event from Lemma~\ref{lem:mab_ucb_event}:
\[
\mathcal{E}
:=\left\{
\forall t\in\{K,\dots,T-1\},\ \forall a\in[K],\ 
|\widehat\mu_t(a)-\mu_a|\le \mathrm{rad}_t(a)
\right\}.
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:mab_ucb_event}, we have $\Pr(\mathcal{E})\ge 1-\delta$.

Condition on $\mathcal{E}$. First note the \emph{optimism} property: for every
$t\ge K$ and every $a\in[K]$,
\[
\mu_a \le \widehat\mu_t(a) + \mathrm{rad}_t(a) = \mathrm{UCB}_t(a).
\]
In particular, $\mu_\star=\mu_{a^\star}\le \mathrm{UCB}_t(a^\star)$.

Since UCB selects $I_t\in\argmax_{a}\mathrm{UCB}_t(a)$, we have
$\mathrm{UCB}_t(I_t)\ge \mathrm{UCB}_t(a^\star)\ge \mu_\star$. Therefore,
\begin{align*}
\mu_\star - \mu_{I_t}
&\le \mathrm{UCB}_t(I_t) - \mu_{I_t}
= \widehat\mu_t(I_t) + \mathrm{rad}_t(I_t) - \mu_{I_t}\\
&\le |\widehat\mu_t(I_t)-\mu_{I_t}| + \mathrm{rad}_t(I_t)
\le 2\,\mathrm{rad}_t(I_t)
= 2\sqrt{\frac{2\ln(2TK/\delta)}{N_t(I_t)}}.
\end{align*}

First, let us provide a distribution-free bound.
Summing over $t=K,\dots,T-1$ gives
\[
R_T
= \sum_{t=0}^{T-1}(\mu_\star-\mu_{I_t})
\le 2K + 2\sqrt{2\ln(2TK/\delta)}\sum_{t=K}^{T-1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_t(I_t)}},
\]
where we used the trivial bound $\mu_\star-\mu_{I_t}\le 2$ for the first $K$ rounds.
Now use the standard counting argument: for each arm $a$,
each time we play $a$ the quantity $N_t(a)$ increases by $1$, hence
\[
\sum_{t=K}^{T-1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_t(I_t)}}
= \sum_{a\in[K]}\sum_{i=1}^{N_T(a)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{i}}
\le \sum_{a\in[K]} 2\sqrt{N_T(a)}
\le 2\sqrt{K\sum_{a}N_T(a)}
= 2\sqrt{KT}.
\]
(We used $\sum_{i=1}^n i^{-1/2}\le 2\sqrt{n}$ and Cauchy--Schwarz.)
Thus,
\[
R_T \le 2K + 8\sqrt{KT\,\ln(2TK/\delta)}.
\]

Now, we provide a gap-dependent bound.
Fix a suboptimal arm $a\neq a^\star$ with gap $\Delta_a>0$.
Suppose that at some round $t\ge K$ the algorithm selects $I_t=a$.
Then
\[
\mathrm{UCB}_t(a)\ge \mathrm{UCB}_t(a^\star)\ge \mu_\star.
\]
On $\mathcal{E}$, we also have $\widehat\mu_t(a)\le \mu_a+\mathrm{rad}_t(a)$, hence
\[
\mathrm{UCB}_t(a) = \widehat\mu_t(a)+\mathrm{rad}_t(a)
\le \mu_a + 2\,\mathrm{rad}_t(a).
\]
Combining the two displays yields
\[
\mu_\star \le \mu_a + 2\,\mathrm{rad}_t(a)
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\Delta_a \le 2\,\mathrm{rad}_t(a)
=2\sqrt{\frac{2\ln(2TK/\delta)}{N_t(a)}},
\]
so necessarily
\[
N_t(a) \le \frac{8\ln(2TK/\delta)}{\Delta_a^2}.
\]
Therefore, the total number of pulls of arm $a$ up to time $T$ satisfies
\[
N_T(a)\le 1 + \frac{8\ln(2TK/\delta)}{\Delta_a^2},
\]
and hence the regret can be bounded as
\[
R_T = \sum_{t=0}^{T-1}(\mu_\star-\mu_{I_t})
= \sum_{a\neq a^\star} \Delta_a\,N_T(a)
\le 2K + \sum_{a\neq a^\star}\left(\Delta_a + \frac{8\ln(2TK/\delta)}{\Delta_a}\right)
\;\le\; O\!\left(K + \sum_{a\neq a^\star}\frac{\ln(2TK/\delta)}{\Delta_a}\right).
\]

To conclude the proof, note that on the event $\mathcal{E}$ (which holds with probability at least $1-\delta$),
we have both bounds, so we may take their minimum.
This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:mab_main}.
\end{proof}

\section{Linear Bandits: Handling Large Action Spaces}
\label{sec:linear_bandits}

Let $D \subset \R^d$ be a compact (but otherwise arbitrary) set of decisions.
On each round $t=0,1,2,\dots$, the learner chooses an action $x_t \in D$ and
observes a reward $r_t \in [-1,1]$.

\paragraph{Linear reward model.}
We assume that, for every history $\mathcal{H}_{<t}$ and every $x\in D$,
the conditional expectation of $r_t$ is linear:
\[
\E[r_t \mid \mathcal{H}_{<t},\, x_t=x] \;=\; \mu^\star \cdot x \in [-1,1],
\]
for some unknown parameter $\mu^\star\in\R^d$.
Equivalently, the noise sequence
\[
\eta_t := r_t - \mu^\star\cdot x_t
\]
is a martingale difference sequence (with respect to the natural filtration).

\paragraph{Regret.}
Let
\[
x^\star \in \argmax_{x\in D}\mu^\star\cdot x,
\]
which exists since $D$ is compact.
We measure performance via the (pseudo-)regret
\[
R_T \;:=\; T\,\mu^\star\cdot x^\star \;-\; \sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mu^\star\cdot x_t.
\]
(Again, one can relate $\sum_{t=0}^{T-1} r_t$ to $\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mu^\star\cdot x_t$
via Hoeffding--Azuma since $(\eta_t)$ is a martingale difference sequence.)

\subsection{The LinUCB algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require regularization $\lambda>0$; confidence radii $(\beta_t)_{t\ge 0}$
\State Initialize $\Sigma_0 \gets \lambda I$ and $b_0 \gets 0$.
\For{$t = 0,1,2,\dots$}
    \State $\widehat{\mu}_t \gets \Sigma_t^{-1} b_t$
    \State \label{alg:ucb_step} Choose
    \[
    x_t \in \argmax_{x\in D}\ \Big( \widehat{\mu}_t\cdot x \;+\; \sqrt{\beta_t}\,\sqrt{x^\top \Sigma_t^{-1}x}\Big)
    \]
    and observe reward $r_t$.
    \State Update $b_{t+1} \gets b_t + r_t x_t$ and $\Sigma_{t+1} \gets \Sigma_t + x_t x_t^\top$.
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\caption{LinUCB (linear bandits with optimistic action selection)}
\label{alg:linUCB}
\end{algorithm}

LinUCB is an ``optimism in the face of uncertainty'' algorithm.
At time $t$, it forms a confidence region (an ellipsoid) around the regularized
least-squares estimate $\widehat{\mu}_t$:
\begin{align*}
\widehat{\mu}_t
&= \arg\min_{\mu\in\R^d}\ \sum_{\tau=0}^{t-1}\big(r_\tau - \mu\cdot x_\tau\big)^2 + \lambda\|\mu\|_2^2
\;=\; \Sigma_t^{-1}\sum_{\tau=0}^{t-1} r_\tau x_\tau,
\end{align*}
where
\[
\Sigma_t = \lambda I + \sum_{\tau=0}^{t-1} x_\tau x_\tau^\top,
\qquad \text{with }\ \Sigma_0=\lambda I.
\]
The confidence set is
\begin{align}\label{eq:ball}
\textsc{Ball}_{t} := \left\{ \mu\in\R^d \;\middle|\;
(\widehat{\mu}_t-\mu)^\top \Sigma_t(\widehat{\mu}_t-\mu) \le \beta_t \right\}.
\end{align}
The action rule in Step~\ref{alg:ucb_step} is exactly
\[
x_t \in \argmax_{x\in D}\ \max_{\mu\in \textsc{Ball}_t} \mu\cdot x,
\]
since $\max_{\mu\in\textsc{Ball}_t}\mu\cdot x = \widehat{\mu}_t\cdot x + \sqrt{\beta_t}\sqrt{x^\top\Sigma_t^{-1}x}$.

\paragraph{Computation.}
Even if we can optimize linear functions over $D$ (i.e., solve $\max_{x\in D}\nu\cdot x$
for any $\nu$), the objective in Step~\ref{alg:ucb_step} includes the (convex) term
$\sqrt{x^\top\Sigma_t^{-1}x}$, so maximizing it over an arbitrary set $D$ need not
be computationally tractable. We return to computational issues and common relaxations
in Section~\ref{bib:bandits}.

\subsection{Upper and Lower Bounds}

Our main message is that one can achieve sublinear regret with only a polynomial
dependence on the dimension $d$, and \emph{no} dependence on $|D|$.

\begin{theorem}\label{theorem:sqrtregret}
Assume $|\mu^\star\cdot x|\le 1$ for all $x\in D$, $\|\mu^\star\|_2\le W$,
$\|x\|_2\le B$ for all $x\in D$, and that the noise $\eta_t$ is conditionally
$\sigma^2$-sub-Gaussian.\footnote{See Definition~\ref{def:sub-gauss}.}
Set
\[
\lambda = \sigma^2/W^2,
\qquad
\beta_t := \sigma^2\Big(2
+ 4 d\log\Big( 1 + \tfrac{tB^2W^2}{ d} \Big)
+ 8\log(4/\delta)\Big).
\]
Then with probability at least $1-\delta$, simultaneously for all $T\ge 1$,
\[
R_T \leq c\, \sigma \sqrt{T}\left( d\log\Big( 1 + \tfrac{TB^2W^2}{ d\sigma^2} \Big)
+ \log(4/\delta) \right),
\]
where $c$ is an absolute constant. In particular, $R_T = \tilde O(d\sqrt{T})$
with high probability.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Lower bound]
There exists a distribution over linear bandit instances (i.e., over $\mu^\star$)
with rewards bounded in $[-1,1]$ and $\sigma^2\le 1$ such that for every (randomized)
algorithm and all $T\ge \max\{256, d^2/16\}$,
\[
\E_{\mu^\star}\,\E[R_T] \ge \frac{1}{2500}\, d\sqrt{T},
\]
where the inner expectation is over the randomness in the interaction (rewards) and the algorithm.
\end{theorem}

We will only prove the upper bound (see Section~\ref{bib:bandits} for pointers).

\paragraph{Remark: $D$-optimal preconditioning.}
The dependence on the a priori bounds $B$ and $W$ in Theorem~\ref{theorem:sqrtregret}
is not intrinsic. One can remove it (up to dimension factors) by working in a
well-chosen coordinate system.

Let $\Sigma_D$ be the $D$-optimal design matrix from Theorem~\ref{lem:d_opt_design},
and define the change of variables
\[
\tilde x := \Sigma_D^{-1/2}x, \qquad \widetilde{\mu}^\star := \Sigma_D^{1/2}\mu^\star.
\]
Then the expected reward is preserved:
$\widetilde{\mu}^\star\cdot \tilde x = \mu^\star\cdot x$.
Moreover, by properties of $\Sigma_D$,
\[
\|\tilde x\| = \|x\|_{\Sigma_D^{-1}} \le \sqrt{d}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\|\widetilde{\mu}^\star\|^2 = (\mu^\star)^\top \Sigma_D \mu^\star
= \E_{x\sim\rho}\big[(\mu^\star\cdot x)^2\big]\le 1,
\]
where the last inequality uses the assumption $|\mu^\star\cdot x|\le 1$ for all $x\in D$.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:linucb_precond}
Assume $|\mu^\star\cdot x|\le 1$ for all $x\in D$ and that the noise $\eta_t$ is
$\sigma^2$ sub-Gaussian. Run LinUCB in the preconditioned coordinates
$\tilde x_t=\Sigma_D^{-1/2}x_t$ with
\[
\lambda=\sigma^2,
\qquad
\beta_t := \sigma^2\Big(2 + 4d\log(1+t) + 8\log(4/\delta)\Big).
\]
Then with probability at least $1-\delta$, simultaneously for all $T\ge 1$,
\[
R_T \le c\,\sigma\sqrt{T}\left(d\log\!\left(1+\frac{T}{\sigma^2}\right)+\log(4/\delta)\right),
\]
for a universal constant $c$.
\end{corollary}


\section{LinUCB Analysis}

The proof of Theorem~\ref{theorem:sqrtregret} decomposes into two steps.  First,
we show that the confidence region $\textsc{Ball}_t$ contains the true parameter
$\mu^\star$ for all times $t$ with high probability.  Second, we bound the regret
on this event via a potential-function argument based on $\log\det(\Sigma_t)$.

\begin{proposition}[Confidence]\label{proposition:confidence}
Let $\delta\in(0,1)$. Under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{theorem:sqrtregret}
(in particular, $\eta_t$ is conditionally $\sigma$-sub-Gaussian), we have
\[
\Pr\!\big(\forall t\ge 0,\ \mu^\star\in \textsc{Ball}_t\big)\ \ge\ 1-\delta.
\]
\end{proposition}

Section~\ref{sec:confidence} proves this by controlling
$\|\widehat{\mu}_t-\mu^\star\|^2_{\Sigma_t}
:=(\widehat{\mu}_t-\mu^\star)^\top \Sigma_t(\widehat{\mu}_t-\mu^\star)$.

\medskip

The second step bounds the regret assuming the above high-probability event.
Define the instantaneous regret
\[
\mathrm{regret}_t \ :=\ (\mu^\star)^\top x^\star - (\mu^\star)^\top x_t,
\qquad\text{so that}\qquad
R_T\ =\ \sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathrm{regret}_t.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Sum of squares regret bound]\label{proposition:l2regretbd}
Assume $\|x\|\le B$ for all $x\in D$, and that $(\beta_t)_{t\ge 0}$ is
nondecreasing with $\beta_t\ge 1$.  For LinUCB, if
$\mu^\star\in \textsc{Ball}_t$ for all $t=0,1,\dots,T-1$, then
\[
\sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \mathrm{regret}_t^2
\ \le\
8\,\beta_T\, d \log\!\left(1+\frac{TB^2}{d\lambda}\right).
\]
\end{proposition}

This is proven in Section~\ref{sec:regret} using a potential function based on
the log-determinant of the ``design'' matrix $\Sigma_t$, which tracks how
accurately we have learned $\mu^\star$ in each direction.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{theorem:sqrtregret}]
On the event $\{\forall t,\ \mu^\star\in \textsc{Ball}_t\}$, Proposition~\ref{proposition:l2regretbd}
and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
R_T
=\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathrm{regret}_t
\le \sqrt{T\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathrm{regret}_t^2}
\le \sqrt{8T\beta_T\, d \log\!\left(1+\frac{TB^2}{d\lambda}\right)}.
\]
Proposition~\ref{proposition:confidence} shows this event holds with probability
at least $1-\delta$.  Plugging in the chosen $\beta_T$ and simplifying yields the stated bound.
\end{proof}

We now prove Propositions~\ref{proposition:l2regretbd} and~\ref{proposition:confidence}.

\subsection{Regret Analysis}\label{sec:regret}

Throughout this section, assume $\mu^\star\in\textsc{Ball}_t$ for all
$t=0,1,\dots,T-1$.  Unless explicitly stated, $\|\cdot\|$ denotes the Euclidean norm.
For a positive definite matrix $A$, we write $\|v\|_A^2 := v^\top A v$.

\begin{lemma}[Ellipsoid width]\label{lemma:width}
For any $x\in D$ and any $\mu\in\textsc{Ball}_t$,
\[
|(\mu-\widehat{\mu}_t)^\top x|
\ \le\
\sqrt{\beta_t\, x^\top \Sigma_t^{-1}x}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
|(\mu-\widehat{\mu}_t)^\top x|
=
|(\Sigma_t^{1/2}(\mu-\widehat{\mu}_t))^\top (\Sigma_t^{-1/2}x)|
\le
\|\mu-\widehat{\mu}_t\|_{\Sigma_t}\ \|x\|_{\Sigma_t^{-1}}.
\]
Since $\mu\in\textsc{Ball}_t$, we have $\|\mu-\widehat{\mu}_t\|_{\Sigma_t}\le \sqrt{\beta_t}$,
and $\|x\|_{\Sigma_t^{-1}}=\sqrt{x^\top \Sigma_t^{-1}x}$.
\end{proof}

Define the normalized width in the chosen direction
\[
w_t \ :=\ \sqrt{x_t^\top \Sigma_t^{-1}x_t}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Regret is controlled by width]\label{lem:regretvswidth}
Fix $t\le T-1$. If $\mu^\star\in\textsc{Ball}_t$ and $\beta_t\ge 1$, then
\[
\mathrm{regret}_t
\le
\min\!\big\{2\sqrt{\beta_t}\, w_t,\ 2\big\}
=
2\min\!\big\{\sqrt{\beta_t}\, w_t,\ 1\big\}
\le
2\sqrt{\beta_T}\min\!\{w_t,1\}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $\mu_t^+\in\arg\max_{\mu\in\textsc{Ball}_t}\mu^\top x_t$.
By the choice of $x_t$ in LinUCB,
\[
(\mu_t^+)^\top x_t
=
\max_{x\in D}\max_{\mu\in\textsc{Ball}_t}\mu^\top x
\ge
(\mu^\star)^\top x^\star,
\]
where the inequality uses $\mu^\star\in\textsc{Ball}_t$.
Therefore,
\[
\mathrm{regret}_t
=(\mu^\star)^\top x^\star-(\mu^\star)^\top x_t
\le
(\mu_t^+-\mu^\star)^\top x_t
\le
|(\mu_t^+-\widehat{\mu}_t)^\top x_t| + |(\widehat{\mu}_t-\mu^\star)^\top x_t|.
\]
Applying Lemma~\ref{lemma:width} to $\mu_t^+$ and to $\mu^\star$ (both in $\textsc{Ball}_t$)
yields $\mathrm{regret}_t\le 2\sqrt{\beta_t}\, w_t$.
Finally, since $|(\mu^\star)^\top x|\le 1$ for all $x\in D$, we always have
$\mathrm{regret}_t\le 2$, giving the stated bound.
\end{proof}

We now relate the sum of widths to a log-determinant potential.

\begin{lemma}[Determinant growth]\label{lem:volumechange}
We have
\[
\det(\Sigma_T)
=
\det(\Sigma_0)\prod_{t=0}^{T-1}(1+w_t^2).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Using $\Sigma_{t+1}=\Sigma_t+x_tx_t^\top$ and the matrix determinant lemma,
\[
\det(\Sigma_{t+1})
=
\det(\Sigma_t)\Big(1+x_t^\top \Sigma_t^{-1}x_t\Big)
=
\det(\Sigma_t)(1+w_t^2).
\]
Iterating from $t=0$ to $T-1$ gives the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Potential function bound]\label{lem:detA}
For any sequence $x_0,\dots,x_{T-1}$ with $\|x_t\|\le B$,
\[
\log\frac{\det(\Sigma_T)}{\det(\Sigma_0)}
=
\log\det\!\left(I+\frac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}x_tx_t^\top\right)
\le
d\log\!\left(1+\frac{TB^2}{d\lambda}\right).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_d$ be the eigenvalues of $\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}x_tx_t^\top$.
Then $\sum_{i=1}^d\sigma_i=\mathrm{Tr}(\sum_t x_tx_t^\top)=\sum_t\|x_t\|^2\le TB^2$.
By AM--GM,
\[
\log\det\!\left(I+\frac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}x_tx_t^\top\right)
=
\sum_{i=1}^d \log\!\left(1+\frac{\sigma_i}{\lambda}\right)
\le
d\log\!\left(1+\frac{1}{d\lambda}\sum_{i=1}^d\sigma_i\right)
\le
d\log\!\left(1+\frac{TB^2}{d\lambda}\right).
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{proposition:l2regretbd}]
Using Lemma~\ref{lem:regretvswidth} and that $\beta_t$ is nondecreasing,
\[
\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathrm{regret}_t^2
\le
\sum_{t=0}^{T-1} 4\beta_t \min\{w_t^2,1\}
\le
4\beta_T \sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\min\{w_t^2,1\}.
\]
Next, for all $u\ge 0$ one has $\min\{u,1\}\le 2\log(1+u)$ (check separately for $u\le 1$ and $u\ge 1$),
so
\[
\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\min\{w_t^2,1\}
\le
2\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\log(1+w_t^2)
=
2\log\frac{\det(\Sigma_T)}{\det(\Sigma_0)},
\]
where the last equality uses Lemma~\ref{lem:volumechange}.
Finally apply Lemma~\ref{lem:detA} to conclude
\[
\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathrm{regret}_t^2
\le
8\beta_T\, d\log\!\left(1+\frac{TB^2}{d\lambda}\right).
\]
\end{proof}

\subsection{Confidence Analysis}\label{sec:confidence}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{proposition:confidence}]
Recall $\Sigma_t=\lambda I+\sum_{\tau=0}^{t-1}x_\tau x_\tau^\top$ and
$\widehat{\mu}_t=\Sigma_t^{-1}\sum_{\tau=0}^{t-1} r_\tau x_\tau$.
Since $r_\tau = (\mu^\star)^\top x_\tau + \eta_\tau$, we have
\[
\widehat{\mu}_t-\mu^\star
=
\Sigma_t^{-1}\sum_{\tau=0}^{t-1}\eta_\tau x_\tau
-\lambda \Sigma_t^{-1}\mu^\star.
\]
Therefore, by the triangle inequality,
\begin{align*}
\|\widehat{\mu}_t-\mu^\star\|_{\Sigma_t}
&\le
\left\|\sum_{\tau=0}^{t-1}\eta_\tau x_\tau\right\|_{\Sigma_t^{-1}}
+
\lambda\|\mu^\star\|_{\Sigma_t^{-1}} \\
&\le
\left\|\sum_{\tau=0}^{t-1}\eta_\tau x_\tau\right\|_{\Sigma_t^{-1}}
+
\sqrt{\lambda}\,\|\mu^\star\|,
\end{align*}
where we used $\Sigma_t\succeq \lambda I$, hence
$\|\mu^\star\|_{\Sigma_t^{-1}}\le \|\mu^\star\|/\lambda^{1/2}$.

Now apply the self-normalized bound (Lemma~\ref{lemma:self_norm}) with
$V_0=\Sigma_0=\lambda I$ and $X_{\tau+1}=x_\tau$.
For any $\delta_t\in(0,1)$, with probability at least $1-\delta_t$,
\[
\left\|\sum_{\tau=0}^{t-1}\eta_\tau x_\tau\right\|_{\Sigma_t^{-1}}
\le
\sigma\sqrt{2\log\!\left(\frac{\det(\Sigma_t)^{1/2}\det(\Sigma_0)^{-1/2}}{\delta_t}\right)}.
\]
Combining the last two displays yields that, with probability at least $1-\delta_t$,
\[
\|\widehat{\mu}_t-\mu^\star\|_{\Sigma_t}
\le
\sqrt{\lambda}\,\|\mu^\star\|
+
\sigma\sqrt{2\log\!\left(\frac{\det(\Sigma_t)^{1/2}\det(\Sigma_0)^{-1/2}}{\delta_t}\right)}.
\]

Choose a summable failure schedule, e.g.
$\delta_t := \frac{6}{\pi^2}\frac{\delta}{(t+1)^2}$ for $t\ge 0$, so that
$\sum_{t=0}^\infty \delta_t \le \delta$.
A union bound then implies that with probability at least $1-\delta$, the above
inequality holds simultaneously for all $t\ge 0$.
With our choice of $\beta_t$ (as in Theorem~\ref{theorem:sqrtregret})
and using the log determinant bound (Lemma~\ref{lem:detA}), this is
equivalent to $\mu^\star\in \textsc{Ball}_t$ for all $t$, proving the proposition.
\end{proof}

\section{Bibliographic Remarks and Further Readings}\label{bib:bandits}

A comprehensive modern reference for multi-armed and linear bandits is the
textbook of \citet{lattimore2020bandit}.

The stochastic $K$-armed bandit model dates back to early work such as
\citet{Rob52}. The first sharp regret guarantees for simple algorithms such as
UCB were developed in \citet{Auer02} (see also the exposition in
\citet{lattimore2020bandit}).


The stochastic linear bandit model was originally introduced by \citet{abelong}.
For stochastic linear bandits, our presentation of LinUCB follows the original
$\sqrt{T}$-regret analysis of \citet{dani2008stochastic}. The confidence-set argument we use is
based on the self-normalized concentration framework of
\citet{abbasi2011improved}, which streamlines the proof and yields clean
high-probability bounds. The minimax lower bound stated here is also due to
\citet{dani2008stochastic}.

\paragraph{Computation.}
LinUCB requires solving, at each round, an optimistic optimization problem over
a confidence ellipsoid. Even when one has an efficient linear optimization
oracle over $D$, this ellipsoidal optimization can be computationally hard for
general action sets $D$ (e.g., for polytopes given by linear inequalities).
A standard workaround (as presented in \citet{dani2008stochastic}) is to replace the $\ell_2$ ellipsoid by a containing
polyhedral (e.g.\ $\ell_1$-type) confidence set, which makes the optimistic step
reducible to finitely many calls to the linear oracle (e.g., by checking
extreme points). This typically incurs an extra $\sqrt{d}$ factor in regret but
yields an implementable algorithm (see \citet{dani2008stochastic,lattimore2020bandit}).
