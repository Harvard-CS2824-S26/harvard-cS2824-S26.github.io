\chapter{Strategic Exploration in Tabular MDPs}
\label{chap:tabular_exploration}

In the generative-model setting, we could query the dynamics at any state--action
pair and reduce reinforcement learning to estimating a collection of local
quantities. In an \emph{online} MDP, the learner has a much more limited interface:
it can only interact with the environment by executing trajectories. As a result,
data are not obtained uniformly over the state space---they are biased toward
states the learner can \emph{reach}.

This makes exploration a central algorithmic challenge. To learn a near-optimal
policy, the agent must sometimes take actions that are not immediately rewarding
in order to gather information about parts of the MDP that are currently uncertain.
The goal is to balance this exploration--exploitation tradeoff so that performance
approaches that of an optimal policy as the number of episodes grows.

In this chapter we focus on \emph{tabular} finite-horizon MDPs and present
\emph{UCB Value Iteration (UCBVI)}, an optimism-based algorithm that achieves
(sublinear) regret. The algorithm combines (i) an empirical transition model
built from trajectory data with (ii) an explicit exploration bonus, and then
plans greedily in the resulting optimistic model.

\section{Setup and regret}
\label{sec:tabular_setup}

We consider a finite-horizon episodic MDP with horizon $H$, discrete state space
$\Scal$ and action space $\Acal$, and a fixed start state $s_0$.
Let $S := |\Scal|$ and $A := |\Acal|$.
The (stage-dependent) transition kernels are $\{P_h^\star(\cdot\mid s,a)\}_{h=0}^{H-1}$.
Rewards are bounded as $r_h(s,a)\in[0,1]$.
For simplicity, we treat rewards as deterministic and known; the extension to unknown
and/or stochastic rewards estimates $r_h$ by an empirical mean and adds an additional
(Hoeffding-style) bonus term.

A learning algorithm runs for $K$ episodes. In episode $k$, the learner selects
a (possibly history-dependent) policy
$\pi^k=\{\pi_h^k\}_{h=0}^{H-1}$ based on the past history $\Hcal_{<k}$, then executes it
to generate a trajectory
\[
s_0^k=s_0,\qquad a_h^k=\pi_h^k(s_h^k),\qquad s_{h+1}^k\sim P_h^\star(\cdot\mid s_h^k,a_h^k),
\qquad h=0,\dots,H-1.
\]

For any policy $\pi$, define the value functions in the true MDP as
\[
V_h^\pi(s)
:= \E\!\left[\sum_{t=h}^{H-1} r_t(s_t,a_t)\ \middle|\ s_h=s,\ \pi,\ P^\star \right],
\qquad
V_h^\star(s):=\max_\pi V_h^\pi(s).
\]
Since rewards lie in $[0,1]$, we have $0\le V_h^\pi(s)\le H-h$ for all $(h,s,\pi)$.

It will be useful later to write expectations along a trajectory using the
\emph{stage-$h$ occupancy measure} of a policy $\pi$:
\[
d_h^\pi(s,a)
\;:=\;
\Pr\!\big(s_h=s,\ a_h=a \ \big|\ s_0,\ \pi,\ P^\star\big),
\qquad h=0,\dots,H-1.
\]

\paragraph{Regret.}
We measure performance using the (pseudo-)regret over $K$ episodes:
\[
R_K
\;:=\;
\E\!\left[\sum_{k=0}^{K-1} \bigl(V_0^\star(s_0)-V_0^{\pi^k}(s_0)\bigr)\right].
\]
Equivalently, since $\E[\sum_{h=0}^{H-1} r_h(s_h^k,a_h^k)\mid \pi^k]=V_0^{\pi^k}(s_0)$,
we may write
\[
R_K
=
\E\!\left[ K V_0^\star(s_0) - \sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\sum_{h=0}^{H-1} r_h(s_h^k,a_h^k)\right].
\]
(When rewards are stochastic, one can relate pseudo-regret to realized cumulative regret
via Hoeffding--Azuma; see Appendix~\ref{chap:concentration}.)

\section{Why exploration must be strategic}
\label{sec:tabular_need_strategic}

\input{Figures/fig_chain_example}
Consider the chain MDP in Figure~\ref{fig:chain}. From the start state $s_0$, there is a
unique action that moves one step to the right; all other actions move left or stay.
A uniformly random policy that picks actions uniformly from $\Acal$ at every step reaches
the rewarding state $s_{H+1}$ only if it chooses the ``move right'' action at each of
the $H$ steps. This happens with probability $(1/A)^H$, which is exponentially small
in the horizon.

In particular, the expected number of episodes before such a policy reaches the rewarding
state even once is on the order of $A^H$. This illustrates a key point: in online RL,
the learner must sometimes \emph{plan} to reach informative states. Exploration is not
only about adding random noise to actions---it must be driven by which parts of the MDP
are currently uncertain and how to reach them.

\section{Algorithm: UCB Value Iteration (UCBVI)}
\label{sec:tabular_ucbvi_alg}

UCBVI is an ``optimism in the face of uncertainty'' algorithm, directly analogous to
UCB for multi-armed bandits (Chapter~\ref{chap:bandits}). The learner:
\begin{enumerate}
\item builds an empirical transition model from the trajectory data collected so far;
\item adds an exploration bonus to encourage actions with high uncertainty;
\item plans optimistically in this modified model via value iteration; and
\item executes the resulting greedy policy for one episode.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Counts and empirical transitions.}
For each stage $h\in\{0,\dots,H-1\}$ and each $(s,a,s')\in\Scal\times\Acal\times\Scal$,
define the pre-episode-$k$ counts
\[
N_h^k(s,a) := \sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\mathbf{1}\{(s_h^i,a_h^i)=(s,a)\},
\qquad
N_h^k(s,a,s') := \sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\mathbf{1}\{(s_h^i,a_h^i,s_{h+1}^i)=(s,a,s')\}.
\]
Define the empirical transition model $\widehat P_h^k(\cdot\mid s,a)$ by
\[
\widehat P_h^k(s'\mid s,a) :=
\begin{cases}
\frac{N_h^k(s,a,s')}{N_h^k(s,a)} & \text{if } N_h^k(s,a)>0,\\[6pt]
\text{any distribution on $\Scal$} & \text{if } N_h^k(s,a)=0.
\end{cases}
\]
When $N_h^k(s,a)=0$, the model $\widehat P_h^k(\cdot\mid s,a)$ is arbitrary; the bonus term
defined next ensures that such state--action pairs are treated as highly uncertain.

\paragraph{Exploration bonus.}
Fix a failure probability $\delta\in(0,1)$ and define the log factor
\[
L := \ln\!\Big(\frac{2SAHK}{\delta}\Big).
\]
For each episode $k$, stage $h$, and $(s,a)$, define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ucbvi_bonus}
b_h^k(s,a)
\;:=\;
\min\left\{H-h,\; 2H\sqrt{\frac{L}{N_h^k(s,a)+1}}\right\}.
\end{equation}
The bonus is large when $(s,a)$ has been visited only a few times and decays like
$1/\sqrt{N_h^k(s,a)}$ as more data are collected. The clipping by $H-h$ matches the
maximum possible remaining return from stage $h$.

\paragraph{Planning (optimistic value iteration).}
Given the empirical model $\{\widehat P_h^k\}$ and bonuses $\{b_h^k\}$, UCBVI performs a
backward dynamic program. Initialize $\widehat V_H^k(\cdot)\equiv 0$ and for
$h=H-1,\dots,0$ compute
\begin{align}
\label{eq:ucbvi_vi}
\widehat Q_h^k(s,a)
&:= r_h(s,a) + b_h^k(s,a) + \sum_{s'\in\Scal}\widehat P_h^k(s'\mid s,a)\,\widehat V_{h+1}^k(s'),\\
\widehat V_h^k(s)
&:= \min\left\{H-h,\ \max_{a\in\Acal}\widehat Q_h^k(s,a)\right\},\qquad
\pi_h^k(s)\in\arg\max_{a\in\Acal}\widehat Q_h^k(s,a).\nonumber
\end{align}
Thus $\pi^k$ is greedy with respect to the optimistic $Q$-values. The truncation
$\widehat V_h^k(s)\le H-h$ simply enforces the natural range of values under bounded rewards.

\paragraph{Execution and updates.}
Finally, execute $\pi^k$ for one episode to obtain $(s_h^k,a_h^k,s_{h+1}^k)_{h=0}^{H-1}$,
then update the counts and repeat.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require failure probability $\delta$
\State Initialize $N_h^0(s,a)\gets 0$ and $N_h^0(s,a,s')\gets 0$ for all $h,s,a,s'$.
\For{$k=0,1,\dots,K-1$}
    \State Form $\widehat P_h^k(\cdot\mid s,a)$ from counts (arbitrary if $N_h^k(s,a)=0$).
    \State Set bonuses $b_h^k(s,a)$ by \eqref{eq:ucbvi_bonus}.
    \State Compute $(\widehat V_h^k,\widehat Q_h^k,\pi_h^k)_{h=0}^{H-1}$ by \eqref{eq:ucbvi_vi}.
    \State Execute $\pi^k$: $s_0^k=s_0$; for $h=0,\dots,H-1$ take $a_h^k=\pi_h^k(s_h^k)$ and sample $s_{h+1}^k\sim P_h^\star(\cdot\mid s_h^k,a_h^k)$.
    \State Update counts: $N_h^{k+1}(\cdot)\gets N_h^k(\cdot)$ then increment the visited $(s_h^k,a_h^k,s_{h+1}^k)$.
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\caption{UCBVI (episodic, tabular)}
\label{alg:ucbvi_clean}
\end{algorithm}

\section{A simple regret analysis}
\label{sec:tabular_ucbvi_analysis_simple}

This section presents a warm-up regret analysis of UCBVI that yields a bound of order
$\widetilde O(H^2 S\sqrt{AK})$. The proof follows a recurring pattern in regret analyses (as in UCB in Chapter~\ref{chap:bandits}) for
optimistic reinforcement learning algorithms: we first establish a time-uniform, high-probability
concentration event controlling the empirical transition estimates; we then use this event to show
\emph{optimism}, namely that the value functions produced by optimistic planning dominate the optimal
value function $V^\star$; next we derive a one-episode \emph{decomposition} that upper bounds the
suboptimality of the executed policy by a sum of exploration bonuses and transition-estimation error
terms along the episode; finally, we apply a simple counting (potential) argument to control the
total contribution of these terms over time via a bound on
$\sum_{k,h} 1/\sqrt{\text{visits}}$ along the realized trajectories.

The dependence on $S$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:ucbvi_simple} is not optimal: the leading term scales like
$S\sqrt{AK}$ rather than $\sqrt{SAK}$. A refined analysis in a Section~\ref{sec:refined_ucbvi} removes this extra factor
by controlling transition errors more sharply than the crude bound
$\|\widehat P-P^\star\|_1\cdot \|V\|_\infty$.

\begin{theorem}[UCBVI regret bound (simple analysis)]
\label{thm:ucbvi_simple}
Run UCBVI (Algorithm~\ref{alg:ucbvi_clean}) with bonuses \eqref{eq:ucbvi_bonus}.
Then for any $\delta\in(0,1)$, letting $L:=\ln\!\big(\tfrac{2SAHK}{\delta}\big)$,
\[
R_K
\;\le\;
O\!\left(H^2 S\sqrt{AK\cdot L}\right)
\;+\;
\delta K H.
\]
In particular, setting $\delta = 1/(KH)$ yields
\[
R_K
\;=\;
\widetilde O\!\left(H^2 S\sqrt{AK}\right).
\]
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{When is this nontrivial?}
Since $0\le V_0^\star(s_0)-V_0^{\pi^k}(s_0)\le H$, we always have the trivial bound $R_K\le KH$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:ucbvi_simple} becomes smaller than $KH$ once $K\gtrsim H^2 S^2 A$ (up to logs),
reflecting that this warm-up argument is still ``model-size'' limited.


\subsection{A time-uniform transition concentration event}

We first record a time-uniform $\ell_1$ concentration bound for empirical transitions.
The key point is that $N_h^k(s,a)$ is random and adaptively chosen; to avoid stopping-time
issues we union bound over all sample sizes $n\in\{1,\dots,K\}$.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform $\ell_1$ transition concentration]
\label{lem:ucbvi_l1}
Fix $\delta\in(0,1)$ and let $L=\ln(2SAHK/\delta)$.
With probability at least $1-\delta/2$, simultaneously for all $h\in[H]$, all $(s,a)\in\Scal\times\Acal$,
and all episodes $k$ with $N_h^k(s,a)\ge 1$,
\[
\big\|\widehat P_h^k(\cdot\mid s,a)-P_h^\star(\cdot\mid s,a)\big\|_1
\;\le\;
2\sqrt{\frac{S L}{N_h^k(s,a)}}.
\]
Consequently, on the same event, for any (possibly data-dependent) $f:\Scal\to[0,H]$,
\[
\left|\big(\widehat P_h^k(\cdot\mid s,a)-P_h^\star(\cdot\mid s,a)\big)^\top f\right|
\;\le\;
2H\sqrt{\frac{S L}{N_h^k(s,a)}}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $(h,s,a)$ and consider the empirical distribution after exactly $n$ i.i.d.\ samples
from $P_h^\star(\cdot\mid s,a)$; denote it by $\widehat P_{h,n}(\cdot\mid s,a)$.
(One can couple the online interaction to an infinite i.i.d.\ sequence for each $(h,s,a)$,
revealing the $n$-th sample on the $n$-th visit; this makes the ``fixed-$n$'' view rigorous.)

By Proposition~\ref{app:discrete} (Appendix), with probability at least $1-\delta'$,
\[
\big\|\widehat P_{h,n}(\cdot\mid s,a)-P_h^\star(\cdot\mid s,a)\big\|_1
\le \sqrt{S}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} + \sqrt{\frac{\ln(1/\delta')}{2n}}\right)
\le 2\sqrt{\frac{S\ln(1/\delta')}{n}},
\]
where the last inequality uses $\ln(1/\delta')\ge \ln 2$ (since $\delta'\le 1/2$ below).
Choose $\delta' := \delta/(2SAHK)$ and union bound over all $(h,s,a)$ and all $n\in\{1,\dots,K\}$.
If $N_h^k(s,a)=n$, then $\widehat P_h^k(\cdot\mid s,a)=\widehat P_{h,n}(\cdot\mid s,a)$,
so the stated bound holds simultaneously for all episodes $k$.

The final display follows from H\"older:
$|(\widehat P-P)^\top f|\le \|\widehat P-P\|_1\|f\|_\infty\le H\|\widehat P-P\|_1$.
\end{proof}

Next we need a sharper bound against the \emph{deterministic} optimal value function
(to prove optimism without paying a $\sqrt{S}$ factor).

\begin{lemma}[Model error against $V^\star$]
\label{lem:ucbvi_vstar}
Fix $\delta\in(0,1)$ and let $L=\ln(2SAHK/\delta)$.
With probability at least $1-\delta/2$, simultaneously for all $h\in[H]$, all $(s,a)$,
and all episodes $k$ with $N_h^k(s,a)\ge 1$,
\[
\left|\big(\widehat P_h^k(\cdot\mid s,a)-P_h^\star(\cdot\mid s,a)\big)^\top V_{h+1}^\star\right|
\;\le\;
H\sqrt{\frac{L}{N_h^k(s,a)}}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $(h,s,a)$ and consider $n$ i.i.d.\ samples $s'_1,\dots,s'_n\sim P_h^\star(\cdot\mid s,a)$.
Let $Y_i := V_{h+1}^\star(s'_i)\in[0,H]$, so $\E[Y_i]=P_h^\star(\cdot\mid s,a)^\top V_{h+1}^\star$.
By Hoeffding's inequality (Lemma~\ref{lem:hoeffding}),
\[
\Pr\!\left(\left|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n Y_i - \E[Y_i]\right|\ge \epsilon\right)
\le 2\exp\!\left(-\frac{2n\epsilon^2}{H^2}\right).
\]
Choosing $\epsilon = H\sqrt{\ln(4SAHK/\delta)/(2n)}$ makes the RHS at most $\delta/(2SAHK)$.
Since $\ln(4SAHK/\delta)\le 2\ln(2SAHK/\delta)=2L$ (using $L\ge \ln 2$), this $\epsilon$
is at most $H\sqrt{L/n}$.
Union bound over all $(h,s,a)$ and $n\in\{1,\dots,K\}$, and translate from $n$ to the
random count $N_h^k(s,a)$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:ucbvi_l1}.
\end{proof}

Let $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{model}}$ denote the intersection of the events in
Lemmas~\ref{lem:ucbvi_l1} and~\ref{lem:ucbvi_vstar}.
Then $\Pr(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{model}})\ge 1-\delta$.

\subsection{Optimism}

\begin{lemma}[Optimism]
\label{lem:ucbvi_optimism}
On $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{model}}$, for every episode $k$, every stage $h$, and every state $s$,
\[
\widehat V_h^k(s)\ \ge\ V_h^\star(s).
\]
In particular, $\widehat V_0^k(s_0)\ge V_0^\star(s_0)$ for all $k$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We prove by backward induction on $h$.
At $h=H$, $\widehat V_H^k\equiv 0=V_H^\star$.

Assume $\widehat V_{h+1}^k(s)\ge V_{h+1}^\star(s)$ for all $s$.
Fix a state $s$ at stage $h$.

If $\widehat V_h^k(s)=H-h$, then $\widehat V_h^k(s)\ge V_h^\star(s)$ holds since $V_h^\star(s)\le H-h$.

Otherwise, $\widehat V_h^k(s)<H-h$. In this case the truncation in \eqref{eq:ucbvi_vi} is inactive,
so $\widehat V_h^k(s)=\max_a \widehat Q_h^k(s,a)$ and therefore $\widehat Q_h^k(s,a)<H-h$ for all $a$.
In particular, no action has a clipped bonus at $(h,s)$, so for every $a$ we have
\[
b_h^k(s,a)=2H\sqrt{\frac{L}{N_h^k(s,a)+1}}
\qquad\text{and hence } N_h^k(s,a)\ge 1.
\]
Let $a\in\Acal$ be arbitrary. Using Lemma~\ref{lem:ucbvi_vstar} and the induction hypothesis,
\begin{align*}
Q_h^\star(s,a)
&= r_h(s,a) + P_h^\star(\cdot\mid s,a)^\top V_{h+1}^\star\\
&\le r_h(s,a) + \widehat P_h^k(\cdot\mid s,a)^\top V_{h+1}^\star
      + H\sqrt{\frac{L}{N_h^k(s,a)}}\\
&\le r_h(s,a) + \widehat P_h^k(\cdot\mid s,a)^\top \widehat V_{h+1}^k
      + H\sqrt{\frac{L}{N_h^k(s,a)}}\\
&\le r_h(s,a) + \widehat P_h^k(\cdot\mid s,a)^\top \widehat V_{h+1}^k
      + b_h^k(s,a)
= \widehat Q_h^k(s,a),
\end{align*}
where the third line uses $\widehat V_{h+1}^k\ge V_{h+1}^\star$ and $\widehat P_h^k$ being a distribution,
and the fourth line uses $b_h^k(s,a)\ge H\sqrt{L/N_h^k(s,a)}$ since $N_h^k(s,a)+1\le 2N_h^k(s,a)$.

Taking maxima over $a$ yields
\[
V_h^\star(s)=\max_a Q_h^\star(s,a)\ \le\ \max_a \widehat Q_h^k(s,a)=\widehat V_h^k(s),
\]
completing the induction.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A one-episode decomposition}

Recall the stage-$h$ occupancy measure of a policy $\pi$ in the true MDP:
\[
d_h^\pi(s,a) := \Pr(s_h=s,a_h=a\mid s_0,\pi,P^\star).
\]

\begin{lemma}[One-episode decomposition]
\label{lem:ucbvi_decomp}
For any episode $k$, the policy $\pi^k$ returned by UCBVI satisfies
\[
\widehat V_0^k(s_0) - V_0^{\pi^k}(s_0)
\le
\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}\E_{(s_h,a_h)\sim d_h^{\pi^k}}\!\left[
b_h^k(s_h,a_h) + \big(\widehat P_h^k(\cdot\mid s_h,a_h)-P_h^\star(\cdot\mid s_h,a_h)\big)^\top \widehat V_{h+1}^k
\right].
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix episode $k$ and abbreviate $\pi=\pi^k$, $\widehat V_h=\widehat V_h^k$, $\widehat P_h=\widehat P_h^k$, $b_h=b_h^k$.
Define $\Delta_h(s):=\widehat V_h(s)-V_h^\pi(s)$, so $\Delta_H\equiv 0$.

For any state $s$ at stage $h$, since $\pi_h(s)\in\arg\max_a \widehat Q_h(s,a)$ and
$\widehat V_h(s)=\min\{H-h,\max_a \widehat Q_h(s,a)\}\le \widehat Q_h(s,\pi_h(s))$, we have
\[
\widehat V_h(s)
\le
r_h(s,\pi_h(s)) + b_h(s,\pi_h(s)) + \widehat P_h(\cdot\mid s,\pi_h(s))^\top \widehat V_{h+1}.
\]
On the other hand,
\[
V_h^\pi(s) = r_h(s,\pi_h(s)) + P_h^\star(\cdot\mid s,\pi_h(s))^\top V_{h+1}^\pi.
\]
Subtracting gives
\[
\Delta_h(s)
\le
b_h(s,\pi_h(s))
+ \big(\widehat P_h(\cdot\mid s,\pi_h(s)) - P_h^\star(\cdot\mid s,\pi_h(s))\big)^\top \widehat V_{h+1}
+ P_h^\star(\cdot\mid s,\pi_h(s))^\top \Delta_{h+1}.
\]
Taking expectations under the trajectory distribution induced by $\pi$ in the true MDP
and unrolling (tower property) from $h=0$ to $H-1$, using $\Delta_H\equiv 0$,
yields the stated bound for $\Delta_0(s_0)=\widehat V_0(s_0)-V_0^\pi(s_0)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A counting lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Counting / potential bound]
\label{lem:ucbvi_counting}
For any sequence of $K$ trajectories $\{(s_h^k,a_h^k)\}_{h=0}^{H-1}$, we have
\[
\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_h^k(s_h^k,a_h^k)+1}}
\;\le\;
2H\sqrt{SAK}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix a stage $h$ and a state--action pair $(s,a)$. Let $n:=N_h^K(s,a)$ be the total number of visits to $(s,a)$ at stage $h$ over all $K$ episodes.
Each time $(s,a)$ is visited, the denominator takes values $\sqrt{1},\sqrt{2},\dots,\sqrt{n}$, so
\[
\sum_{\substack{k:\ (s_h^k,a_h^k)=(s,a)}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_h^k(s,a)+1}}
\le \sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\sqrt{i}} \le 2\sqrt{n}.
\]
Summing over all $(s,a)$ and using Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_h^k(s_h^k,a_h^k)+1}}
\le
2\sum_{s,a}\sqrt{N_h^K(s,a)}
\le
2\sqrt{SA\sum_{s,a}N_h^K(s,a)}
=
2\sqrt{SA\cdot K}.
\]
Finally sum over $h=0,\dots,H-1$ to get $2H\sqrt{SAK}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:ucbvi_simple}}

\begin{proof}
Fix $\delta\in(0,1)$ and let $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{model}}$ be as above, with
$\Pr(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{model}})\ge 1-\delta$.

First, we will reduce the regret to the optimistic planning gap.
On $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{model}}$, optimism (Lemma~\ref{lem:ucbvi_optimism}) implies
\[
V_0^\star(s_0) - V_0^{\pi^k}(s_0)
\le
\widehat V_0^k(s_0) - V_0^{\pi^k}(s_0).
\]

We now decompose one episode and bound the model error term.
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:ucbvi_decomp} and upper bound the model error term by absolute value:
\[
\widehat V_0^k(s_0) - V_0^{\pi^k}(s_0)
\le
\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}\E_{(s_h,a_h)\sim d_h^{\pi^k}}\!\left[
b_h^k(s_h,a_h) + \left|\big(\widehat P_h^k(\cdot\mid s_h,a_h)-P_h^\star(\cdot\mid s_h,a_h)\big)^\top \widehat V_{h+1}^k\right|
\right].
\]
Since $\|\widehat V_{h+1}^k\|_\infty\le H$, Lemma~\ref{lem:ucbvi_l1} gives (for $N_h^k(s_h,a_h)\ge 1$)
\[
\left|\big(\widehat P_h^k-P_h^\star\big)^\top \widehat V_{h+1}^k\right|
\le
2H\sqrt{\frac{S L}{N_h^k(s_h,a_h)}}
\le
2\sqrt{2}\,H\sqrt{\frac{S L}{N_h^k(s_h,a_h)+1}},
\]
where we used $N+1\le 2N$ for $N\ge 1$.
If $N_h^k(s_h,a_h)=0$, then trivially
$\left|\big(\widehat P_h^k-P_h^\star\big)^\top \widehat V_{h+1}^k\right|\le \|\widehat V_{h+1}^k\|_\infty\le H$,
and since $L\ge \ln 2$ we still have
$H\le 2\sqrt{2}\,H\sqrt{SL/(0+1)}$.
Thus, in all cases,
\[
\left|\big(\widehat P_h^k(\cdot\mid s_h,a_h)-P_h^\star(\cdot\mid s_h,a_h)\big)^\top \widehat V_{h+1}^k\right|
\le
2\sqrt{2}\,H\sqrt{\frac{S L}{N_h^k(s_h,a_h)+1}}.
\]
Also, by the bonus definition \eqref{eq:ucbvi_bonus} and $S\ge 1$,
\[
b_h^k(s_h,a_h)\le 2H\sqrt{\frac{L}{N_h^k(s_h,a_h)+1}}
\le 2H\sqrt{\frac{S L}{N_h^k(s_h,a_h)+1}}.
\]
Combining the last two displays yields, on $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{model}}$,
\[
V_0^\star(s_0) - V_0^{\pi^k}(s_0)
\le
(2+2\sqrt{2})H\sqrt{SL}\cdot
\E\!\left[\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_h^k(s_h^k,a_h^k)+1}}\ \middle|\ \Hcal_{<k}\right].
\]

Next, we sum over episodes using the counting lemma.
Summing over $k$ and taking total expectation, then applying Lemma~\ref{lem:ucbvi_counting}, gives
\[
\E\!\left[\mathbf{1}\{\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{model}}\}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\big(V_0^\star(s_0)-V_0^{\pi^k}(s_0)\big)\right]
\le
(2+2\sqrt{2})H\sqrt{SL}\cdot
\E\!\left[\sum_{k,h}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_h^k(s_h^k,a_h^k)+1}}\right]
\le
O\!\left(H^2 S\sqrt{AK\cdot L}\right).
\]
On the complement $\overline{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{model}}$, use $0\le V_0^\star(s_0)-V_0^{\pi^k}(s_0)\le H$ to obtain
\[
\E\!\left[\mathbf{1}\{\overline{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{model}}\}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\big(V_0^\star(s_0)-V_0^{\pi^k}(s_0)\big)\right]
\le
\Pr(\overline{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{model}})\cdot KH
\le
\delta K H.
\]
Combining completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\section{An Improved Regret Bound}
\label{sec:refined_ucbvi}

The simple analysis in Theorem~\ref{thm:ucbvi_simple} bounds the model error term
$\big(\widehat P_h^k-P_h^\star\big)^\top \widehat V_{h+1}^k$ by
$\|\widehat P_h^k-P_h^\star\|_1\cdot \|\widehat V_{h+1}^k\|_\infty$, which incurs an
extraneous $\sqrt S$ factor. This section refines the argument and improves the leading
dependence to $\widetilde O\!\big(H^2\sqrt{SAK}\big)$, at the cost of an additional lower-order term.
The leading dependence on $(S,A,K)$ is optimal up to factors depending on $H$ (see Chapter~\ref{chap:generative}
for related sample-complexity results with a generative model). We discuss minimax regret rates and
sharper bounds in Section~\ref{ucbvi:bib}.

\begin{theorem}[UCBVI regret bound (refined analysis)]
\label{thm:ucbvi_refined}
Run UCBVI (Algorithm~\ref{alg:ucbvi_clean}) with bonuses \eqref{eq:ucbvi_bonus}.
There exists a universal constant $C>0$ such that for any $\delta\in(0,1)$, letting
\[
L \;:=\; \ln\!\Big(\frac{2S^2AHK}{\delta}\Big),
\]
the expected regret satisfies
\[
R_K
\;\le\;
C\!\left(
H^2\sqrt{SAK\cdot L}
\;+\;
H^3 S^2A\cdot L\cdot \ln(1+K)
\right)
\;+\;
\delta K H.
\]
In particular, setting $\delta=1/(KH)$ yields
$
R_K=\widetilde O\!\big(H^2\sqrt{SAK}+H^3S^2A\big).
$
\end{theorem}

The proof follows the same template as in Section~\ref{sec:tabular_ucbvi_analysis_simple}:
optimism, a one-episode decomposition, and counting. The main new ingredient is a Bernstein/Freedman-style
bound that controls
$\big(\widehat P_h^k-P_h^\star\big)^\top(\widehat V_{h+1}^k-V_{h+1}^\star)$
without paying $\sqrt S$ in the leading term.

\subsection{A Bernstein-style transition bound}

We begin with a pointwise empirical Bernstein bound for each next-state probability. It can be proved by
applying Freedman's inequality (Lemma~\ref{lem:freedman}) to the martingale differences associated with
indicator variables and then taking a union bound over $(h,s,a,s',k)$.

\begin{lemma}[Pointwise empirical Bernstein bound]
\label{lem:ucbvi_pointwise_bern}
Fix $\delta\in(0,1)$ and let $L=\ln(2S^2AHK/\delta)$.
With probability at least $1-\delta$, simultaneously for all stages $h\in\{0,\dots,H-1\}$,
all $(s,a)\in\Scal\times\Acal$, all $s'\in\Scal$, and all episodes $k\in\{0,\dots,K-1\}$,
\[
\big|\widehat P_h^k(s'\mid s,a)-P_h^\star(s'\mid s,a)\big|
\;\le\;
2\sqrt{\frac{P_h^\star(s'\mid s,a)\,L}{N_h^k(s,a)+1}}
\;+\;
\frac{4L}{N_h^k(s,a)+1}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $(h,s,a,s')$ and abbreviate $p:=P_h^\star(s'\mid s,a)$.
For episode index $i\in\{0,\dots,K-1\}$ define
\[
X_i \;:=\; \mathbf{1}\{(s_h^i,a_h^i,s_{h+1}^i)=(s,a,s')\} \;-\; \mathbf{1}\{(s_h^i,a_h^i)=(s,a)\}\,p.
\]
Then $(X_i)_{i\ge 0}$ is a martingale difference sequence with $|X_i|\le 1$, and
\[
\sum_{i=0}^{k-1} X_i \;=\; N_h^k(s,a,s') - p\,N_h^k(s,a).
\]
Moreover,
\[
\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\E[X_i^2\mid \mathcal{H}_{<i}]
\;=\;
p(1-p)\,N_h^k(s,a)
\;\le\;
p\,N_h^k(s,a).
\]
Freedman's inequality (Lemma~\ref{lem:freedman}) and a standard inversion imply that with probability at least $1-\delta'$,
simultaneously for all $k$,
\[
\big|N_h^k(s,a,s') - p\,N_h^k(s,a)\big|
\;\le\;
\sqrt{2p\,N_h^k(s,a)\ln(1/\delta')}
\;+\;
\frac{2}{3}\ln(1/\delta').
\]
Divide by $N_h^k(s,a)\vee 1$ and use $N_h^k(s,a)\vee 1 \ge (N_h^k(s,a)+1)/2$ to obtain the stated bound
(with slightly loosened constants). Finally, set $\delta'=\delta/(S^2AHK)$ and take a union bound over
all $(h,s,a,s')$.
\end{proof}

A convenient consequence is a \emph{self-bounding} inequality for linear functionals of the form
$(\widehat P-P^\star)^\top f$ when $f$ is nonnegative. This is the key step that ultimately yields
the improved $\sqrt{SAK}$ dependence.

\begin{lemma}[Self-bounding transition error]
\label{lem:ucbvi_self_bounding}
On the event of Lemma~\ref{lem:ucbvi_pointwise_bern}, for all $h,k,s,a$ and all functions
$f:\Scal\to[0,H]$,
\[
\left|\big(\widehat P_h^k(\cdot\mid s,a)-P_h^\star(\cdot\mid s,a)\big)^\top f\right|
\;\le\;
\frac{P_h^\star(\cdot\mid s,a)^\top f}{H}
\;+\;
c_h^k(s,a),
\qquad
c_h^k(s,a):=\frac{c\,H^2SL}{N_h^k(s,a)+1},
\]
for a universal constant $c>0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $(h,k,s,a)$ and abbreviate $\widehat P:=\widehat P_h^k(\cdot\mid s,a)$,
$P:=P_h^\star(\cdot\mid s,a)$, and $N:=N_h^k(s,a)$.
Using Lemma~\ref{lem:ucbvi_pointwise_bern} and $f\ge 0$,
\[
\big|(\widehat P-P)^\top f\big|
\le \sum_{s'} f(s')\big|\widehat P(s')-P(s')\big|
\le T_1 + T_2,
\]
where
\[
T_1 := \sum_{s'} f(s')\cdot 2\sqrt{\frac{P(s')\,L}{N+1}},
\qquad
T_2 := \sum_{s'} f(s')\cdot \frac{4L}{N+1}.
\]
For the linear term, $T_2\le \frac{4SHL}{N+1}\le \frac{4H^2SL}{N+1}$ (since $H\ge 1$).
For the square-root term, by Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
T_1
\le
2\sqrt{\frac{L}{N+1}}\sum_{s'}\sqrt{P(s')\,f(s')^2}
\le
2\sqrt{\frac{SL}{N+1}}\sqrt{\sum_{s'}P(s')\,f(s')^2}
=
2\sqrt{\frac{SL}{N+1}}\sqrt{P f^2}.
\]
Since $0\le f\le H$, we have $f^2\le H f$ and thus $Pf^2\le H\,Pf$, giving
\[
T_1 \le 2\sqrt{\frac{HSL}{N+1}}\sqrt{Pf}
= \sqrt{\frac{4H^2SL}{N+1}\cdot \frac{Pf}{H}}.
\]
Applying $\sqrt{uv}\le \tfrac{u+v}{2}$ with $u=(Pf)/H$ and $v=4H^2SL/(N+1)$ yields
\[
T_1 \le \frac{Pf}{H} + \frac{2H^2SL}{N+1}.
\]
Combining the bounds on $T_1$ and $T_2$ proves the claim (absorbing constants into $c$).
\end{proof}

\subsection{A recursive control of the optimistic gap}

For each episode $k$ define the optimistic gap
\[
\Delta_h^k(s) := \widehat V_h^k(s)-V_h^\star(s)\ \ge\ 0,
\]
where nonnegativity holds on $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{model}}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:ucbvi_optimism}.
The next lemma shows that $\Delta_h^k$ satisfies a useful recursion.

\begin{lemma}[Gap recursion]
\label{lem:ucbvi_gap_recursion}
Assume $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{model}}$ holds (so $\widehat V^k\ge V^\star$) and
Lemma~\ref{lem:ucbvi_self_bounding} holds.
Then for every episode $k$, stage $h$, and state $s$, letting $a=\pi_h^k(s)$,
\[
\Delta_h^k(s)
\;\le\;
2b_h^k(s,a) + c_h^k(s,a)
\;+\;
\Big(1+\frac{1}{H}\Big)\,
\E_{s'\sim P_h^\star(\cdot\mid s,a)}\!\big[\Delta_{h+1}^k(s')\big].
\]
Consequently, for every $h$ and $s$,
\begin{align*}
\Delta_h^k(s)
\; & \le\;
\E\!\left[\sum_{\tau=h}^{H-1}\Big(1+\frac{1}{H}\Big)^{\tau-h}\big(2b_\tau^k(s_\tau,a_\tau)+c_\tau^k(s_\tau,a_\tau)\big)
\ \middle|\ s_h=s,\ \pi^k\right]\\
\; & \le\;
e\cdot
\E\!\left[\sum_{\tau=h}^{H-1}\big(2b_\tau^k(s_\tau,a_\tau)+c_\tau^k(s_\tau,a_\tau)\big)\ \middle|\ s_h=s,\ \pi^k\right].
\end{align*}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $(k,h,s)$ and let $a=\pi_h^k(s)$. Using $\widehat V_h^k(s)\le \widehat Q_h^k(s,a)$ and
$V_h^\star(s)\ge Q_h^\star(s,a)$,
\[
\Delta_h^k(s)\le \widehat Q_h^k(s,a)-Q_h^\star(s,a)
= b_h^k(s,a) + \widehat P_h^k(\cdot\mid s,a)^\top \widehat V_{h+1}^k
- P_h^\star(\cdot\mid s,a)^\top V_{h+1}^\star.
\]
Write $\widehat V_{h+1}^k=V_{h+1}^\star+\Delta_{h+1}^k$ and expand:
\[
\Delta_h^k(s)\le b_h^k(s,a)
+ \big(\widehat P_h^k-P_h^\star\big)^\top V_{h+1}^\star
+ \widehat P_h^k{}^\top \Delta_{h+1}^k.
\]
On $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{model}}$, Lemma~\ref{lem:ucbvi_vstar} bounds the middle term, and by the
choice of bonus (and the fact that $\ln(2S^2AHK/\delta)\le 2\ln(2SAHK/\delta)$), it can be absorbed
into $b_h^k(s,a)$ up to a constant factor. This yields an additional $+b_h^k(s,a)$ term.

For the remaining term, write
\[
\widehat P_h^k{}^\top \Delta_{h+1}^k
=
P_h^\star{}^\top \Delta_{h+1}^k
+
\big(\widehat P_h^k-P_h^\star\big)^\top \Delta_{h+1}^k,
\]
and bound the last inner product by Lemma~\ref{lem:ucbvi_self_bounding} with $f=\Delta_{h+1}^k$.
Since $P_h^\star{}^\top \Delta_{h+1}^k=\E_{s'\sim P_h^\star(\cdot\mid s,a)}[\Delta_{h+1}^k(s')]$, this gives
the stated one-step recursion.

Unrolling the recursion gives the weighted sum, and the final inequality uses
$\big(1+\tfrac{1}{H}\big)^{m}\le e$ for all integers $m\ge 0$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Per-episode regret bound and summation}

The refined recursion allows us to bound the one-episode regret by the same terms
$2b+c$ (up to a constant factor), without ever using $\|\widehat P-P^\star\|_1\cdot \|\widehat V\|_\infty$.

\begin{lemma}[Per-episode refined bound]
\label{lem:ucbvi_refined_episode}
Assume $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{model}}$ and Lemmas~\ref{lem:ucbvi_self_bounding}--\ref{lem:ucbvi_gap_recursion} hold.
Then for every episode $k$,
\[
V_0^\star(s_0)-V_0^{\pi^k}(s_0)
\;\le\;
e\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}\E_{(s_h,a_h)\sim d_h^{\pi^k}}\!\left[2b_h^k(s_h,a_h)+c_h^k(s_h,a_h)\right].
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix episode $k$ and abbreviate $\pi=\pi^k$. On $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{model}}$, optimism
(Lemma~\ref{lem:ucbvi_optimism}) implies
$V_0^\star(s_0)-V_0^\pi(s_0)\le \widehat V_0^k(s_0)-V_0^\pi(s_0)$.
Apply the one-episode decomposition (Lemma~\ref{lem:ucbvi_decomp}):
\[
\widehat V_0^k(s_0)-V_0^\pi(s_0)
\le
\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}\E_{d_h^{\pi}}\!\left[b_h^k + (\widehat P_h^k-P_h^\star)^\top \widehat V_{h+1}^k\right].
\]
Write $\widehat V_{h+1}^k=V_{h+1}^\star+\Delta_{h+1}^k$ to obtain
\[
(\widehat P_h^k-P_h^\star)^\top \widehat V_{h+1}^k
=
(\widehat P_h^k-P_h^\star)^\top V_{h+1}^\star
+
(\widehat P_h^k-P_h^\star)^\top \Delta_{h+1}^k.
\]
The first term is bounded by $b_h^k$ using Lemma~\ref{lem:ucbvi_vstar} (and the bonus definition),
so
\[
\widehat V_0^k(s_0)-V_0^\pi(s_0)
\le
\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}\E_{d_h^{\pi}}\!\left[2b_h^k + (\widehat P_h^k-P_h^\star)^\top \Delta_{h+1}^k\right].
\]
Next, use Lemma~\ref{lem:ucbvi_self_bounding} with $f=\Delta_{h+1}^k$:
\[
(\widehat P_h^k-P_h^\star)^\top \Delta_{h+1}^k
\le
\frac{P_h^\star{}^\top \Delta_{h+1}^k}{H} + c_h^k.
\]
Let $\alpha_h^k(s,a):=2b_h^k(s,a)+c_h^k(s,a)$. Combining the last two displays yields
\[
\widehat V_0^k(s_0)-V_0^\pi(s_0)
\le
\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}\E_{d_h^{\pi}}\!\left[\alpha_h^k\right]
\;+\;
\frac{1}{H}\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}\E_{d_h^{\pi}}\!\left[P_h^\star{}^\top \Delta_{h+1}^k\right].
\]
By the tower property, $\E_{d_h^\pi}[P_h^\star{}^\top \Delta_{h+1}^k]=\E[\Delta_{h+1}^k(s_{h+1})]$ under $\pi$.
Using the unrolled bound in Lemma~\ref{lem:ucbvi_gap_recursion} and exchanging sums,
\begin{align*}
\frac{1}{H}\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}\E\!\left[\Delta_{h+1}^k(s_{h+1})\right]
&\le
\frac{1}{H}\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}\sum_{\tau=h+1}^{H-1}\Big(1+\frac{1}{H}\Big)^{\tau-h-1}\E_{d_\tau^\pi}\!\left[\alpha_\tau^k\right] \\
&=
\sum_{\tau=0}^{H-1}\E_{d_\tau^\pi}\!\left[\alpha_\tau^k\right]\cdot
\frac{1}{H}\sum_{j=0}^{\tau-1}\Big(1+\frac{1}{H}\Big)^{j}
%\;\le\;                                                                                                                                 %\sum_{\tau=0}^{H-1}\E_{d_\tau^\pi}\!\left[\alpha_\tau^k\right]
                                                                                                                                   %\cdot\Big(\Big(1+\frac{1}{H}\Big)^{\tau}-1\Big)
\;\le\;
(e-1)\sum_{\tau=0}^{H-1}\E_{d_\tau^\pi}\!\left[\alpha_\tau^k\right],
\end{align*}
where we used the geometric-series identity and $(1+1/H)^\tau\le e$.
Substituting back gives
\[
\widehat V_0^k(s_0)-V_0^\pi(s_0)
\le
e\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}\E_{d_h^{\pi}}\!\left[\alpha_h^k\right],
\]
which is the stated bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:ucbvi_refined}]
Let $\mathcal{E}$ be the intersection of $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{model}}$ and the event in
Lemma~\ref{lem:ucbvi_pointwise_bern}, each invoked with failure probability $\delta/2$.
Then $\Pr(\mathcal{E})\ge 1-\delta$, and on $\mathcal{E}$ all assumptions of
Lemma~\ref{lem:ucbvi_refined_episode} hold.

Decompose the expected regret as
\[
R_K
=
\E\!\left[\mathbf{1}\{\mathcal{E}\}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\big(V_0^\star(s_0)-V_0^{\pi^k}(s_0)\big)\right]
+
\E\!\left[\mathbf{1}\{\overline{\mathcal{E}}\}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\big(V_0^\star(s_0)-V_0^{\pi^k}(s_0)\big)\right].
\]
On $\mathcal{E}$, Lemma~\ref{lem:ucbvi_refined_episode} and the law of total expectation yield
\[
\E\!\left[\mathbf{1}\{\mathcal{E}\}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\big(V_0^\star(s_0)-V_0^{\pi^k}(s_0)\big)\right]
\le
e\,\E\!\left[\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}\big(2b_h^k(s_h^k,a_h^k)+c_h^k(s_h^k,a_h^k)\big)\right].
\]
For the bonus term, using $b_h^k(s,a)\le 2H\sqrt{L/(N_h^k(s,a)+1)}$ and the counting lemma
(Lemma~\ref{lem:ucbvi_counting}),
\[
\sum_{k,h}2b_h^k(s_h^k,a_h^k)
\le
4H\sqrt{L}\sum_{k,h}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_h^k(s_h^k,a_h^k)+1}}
\le
8H^2\sqrt{SAK\cdot L}.
\]
For the $c$ term, since $c_h^k(s,a)=\frac{cH^2SL}{N_h^k(s,a)+1}$,
\[
\sum_{k,h}c_h^k(s_h^k,a_h^k)
=
cH^2SL\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}\sum_{s,a}\sum_{i=1}^{N_h^K(s,a)}\frac{1}{i}
\le
cH^2SL\cdot (HSA)\cdot \ln(1+K)
=
O\!\big(H^3S^2A\cdot L\cdot \ln(1+K)\big).
\]
On $\overline{\mathcal{E}}$, we use the trivial bound $0\le V_0^\star(s_0)-V_0^{\pi^k}(s_0)\le H$ to obtain
\[
\E\!\left[\mathbf{1}\{\overline{\mathcal{E}}\}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\big(V_0^\star(s_0)-V_0^{\pi^k}(s_0)\big)\right]
\le
\Pr(\overline{\mathcal{E}})\cdot KH
\le
\delta K H.
\]
Combining the bounds proves the theorem.
\end{proof}

\section{Bibliographic Remarks and Further Readings}\label{ucbvi:bib}

This chapter studies strategic exploration in finite-horizon episodic tabular MDPs
under the regret criterion. The main algorithmic template is \emph{optimism + planning}:
construct an empirical MDP from trajectory data, add an exploration bonus to encourage
visiting uncertain state--action pairs, and plan in the resulting optimistic model.
Our presentation and proofs follow \citet{pmlr-v70-azar17a} (see also the related
treatment of \citet{DBLP:conf/nips/DannLB17}). We first give a simplified
analysis of UCBVI yielding a regret bound of order $H^2 S\sqrt{AK}$ (up to logarithmic
factors), and then sketch a refined analysis that improves the leading dependence on
$S$ to obtain $H^2\sqrt{SAK}$ (again up to logarithmic factors), at the cost of an
additional lower-order term.


The first provably sample-efficient exploration algorithms for finite MDPs were
developed under the PAC criterion. Classic examples include E$^3$ \citep{kearns2002optimal}
and R-max \citep{brafman2002r}, which formalize optimism via either explicit
``known/unknown'' reasoning or optimistic models. The thesis of \citep{kakade2003sample}
gives a broader treatment and refinements, including improved sample complexity bounds in
the tabular setting.

In the regret framework, an influential starting point for continuing (average-reward)
MDPs is UCRL2 \citep{jaksch2010optimal}, which established $\tilde O(\sqrt{T})$ regret as a
function of the time horizon $T$ (with additional dependence on structural parameters such
as the diameter). The $\sqrt{T}$ scaling is minimax-optimal in $T$, while sharpening the
dependence on other problem parameters has motivated substantial follow-up work.

For episodic finite-horizon MDPs (the setting of this chapter), \citet{pmlr-v70-azar17a}
gives near-minimax regret guarantees for UCBVI-type algorithms. A complementary perspective
that unifies PAC and regret analyses via \emph{Uniform-PAC} guarantees is developed by
\citet{DBLP:conf/nips/DannLB17}.

There is also a long line of work on model-free learning. Early finite-sample analyses of
Q-learning and related ``indirect'' methods appear in \citet{DBLP:conf/nips/KearnsS98a} (in a generative model setting).
On the PAC side, \citet{strehl2006pac} provides a model-free exploration algorithm with
provable sample-efficiency guarantees. For regret guarantees in episodic MDPs with
model-free learning, \citet{jin2018qlearning} shows that Q-learning with UCB-style
exploration achieves $\tilde O(\sqrt{H^3 S A T})$ regret (where $T=KH$), matching the
optimal $\sqrt{T}$ dependence and coming within a $\sqrt{H}$ factor (up to logs) of the
best-known model-based rates.

An alternative to optimism is posterior sampling (Thompson sampling) for MDPs.
Posterior Sampling for RL (PSRL) was studied by \citet{osband2013psrl}, and further
finite-horizon analyses and comparisons between posterior sampling and optimism are given
by \citet{pmlr-v70-osband17a}.

Lower bounds for regret in tabular reinforcement learning, and further discussion of the
minimax benchmark, can be found in \citet{pmlr-v70-azar17a,osband2016on}. We also refer to \citet{dann2015sample} for lower bounds in closely related finite-horizon settings.
