\chapter{Optimality}

In this chapter we study global convergence properties of policy gradient methods when
the gradients are computed exactly. We largely restrict attention to the tabular softmax
policy class from Example~\ref{ex:softmax}.

Given a start-state distribution $\rho\in \Delta(\Scal)$, our ultimate objective is
\[
\max_{\theta\in\Theta} V^{\pi_\theta}(\rho).
\]
As we will see, it is often helpful to optimize under a different start-state distribution
$\mu$, even when performance is ultimately measured under $\rho$:
\[
\max_{\theta\in\Theta} V^{\pi_\theta}(\mu).
\]

Recall the normalized discounted state-visitation distribution
\[
d_\mu^\pi(s)
:=
(1-\gamma)\E_{s_0\sim\mu}\Big[\sum_{t=0}^\infty \gamma^t \mathbf{1}\{s_t=s\}\Big].
\]
We will frequently use the fact that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dpi_lb_mu}
d_\mu^\pi(s)\ge (1-\gamma)\mu(s)
\qquad \forall s\in\Scal.
\end{equation}

We study three algorithms for the softmax policy class:
the vanilla policy gradient method, a log-barrier regularized variant,
and the natural policy gradient method. The main message is that
first-order stationarity alone is not enough for global optimality,
but additional structure can restore strong guarantees.

\section{Vanishing gradients and the need for coverage}
\label{sec:vanishing-gradients}

Before turning to the softmax policy class, it is helpful to isolate the basic difficulty:
small gradients need not imply near-optimality. If a policy rarely visits rewarding states,
then the gradient can be very small simply because the learning signal almost never appears.

This issue already arises in the chain MDP of Figure~\ref{fig:chain}. The starting state is
$s_0$, the discount factor is $\gamma = H/(H+1)$, and we use the direct parameterization
\[
\pi_\theta(a_i|s)=\theta_{s,a_i},\qquad i=1,2,3,
\qquad
\pi_\theta(a_4|s)=1-\theta_{s,a_1}-\theta_{s,a_2}-\theta_{s,a_3}.
\]
Thus the parameter vector has dimension $3|\Scal|$ and we do not over-parameterize the policy.

If the action corresponding to moving right is assigned probability less than $1/4$ at every
state, then the probability of reaching the rewarding end of the chain is exponentially small
in $H$. The next proposition records a quantitative form of this phenomenon.

\begin{proposition}[Vanishing gradients at suboptimal parameters]
\label{proposition:small_grad}
Consider the chain MDP of Figure~\ref{fig:chain}, with $H+2$ states, discount factor
$\gamma = H/(H+1)$, and the direct policy parameterization above.
Suppose $\theta$ satisfies $0<\theta<1$ componentwise and
\[
\theta_{s,a_1}<\frac14
\qquad \text{for all } s\in\Scal.
\]
Then for all
\[
k \le \frac{H}{40\log(2H)}-1,
\]
we have
\[
\|\nabla_\theta^k V^{\pi_\theta}(s_0)\|
\le
(1/3)^{H/4},
\]
where $\nabla_\theta^k V^{\pi_\theta}(s_0)$ is the $k$th derivative tensor and the norm is the
operator norm. Furthermore,
\[
V^\star(s_0)-V^{\pi_\theta}(s_0)
\ge
\frac{H+1}{8} - \frac{(H+1)^2}{3^H}.
\]
\end{proposition}

We omit the proof; see the bibliographic notes. The proposition shows that even exact gradients,
and indeed many higher-order derivatives, can be exponentially small at highly suboptimal parameters.
Thus generic non-convex optimization guarantees do not by themselves imply global convergence.

\section{Policy gradient ascent with softmax policies}

We now return to the softmax parameterization
\[
\pi_\theta(a|s)=\frac{\exp(\theta_{s,a})}{\sum_{a'}\exp(\theta_{s,a'})}.
\]
The following coordinate formula is immediate from differentiation:
\[
\frac{\partial \log \pi_\theta(a|s)}{\partial \theta_{s',a'}}
=
\mathbf{1}\{s=s'\}\Big(\mathbf{1}\{a=a'\}-\pi_\theta(a'|s)\Big).
\]

Combining this with the advantage form of the policy gradient from
Theorem~\ref{thm:pg_expressions} gives a particularly useful identity.

\begin{lemma}\label{eq:softmax-grad}
For the softmax policy class,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:softmax_grad}
\frac{\partial V^{\pi_\theta}(\mu)}{\partial \theta_{s,a}}
=
\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\,
d_\mu^{\pi_\theta}(s)\,\pi_\theta(a|s)\,A^{\pi_\theta}(s,a).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Using the advantage expression in Theorem~\ref{thm:pg_expressions},
\begin{align*}
\frac{\partial V^{\pi_\theta}(\mu)}{\partial \theta_{s',a'}}
&=
\frac{1}{1-\gamma}
\E_{s\sim d_\mu^{\pi_\theta}}
\E_{a\sim \pi_\theta(\cdot|s)}
\Big[
A^{\pi_\theta}(s,a)
\frac{\partial \log \pi_\theta(a|s)}{\partial \theta_{s',a'}}
\Big] \\
&=
\frac{1}{1-\gamma}
d_\mu^{\pi_\theta}(s')
\E_{a\sim \pi_\theta(\cdot|s')}
\Big[
A^{\pi_\theta}(s',a)
\big(\mathbf{1}\{a=a'\}-\pi_\theta(a'|s')\big)
\Big].
\end{align*}
The first term in the expectation is
\[
\pi_\theta(a'|s')A^{\pi_\theta}(s',a'),
\]
and the second is
\[
\pi_\theta(a'|s')\sum_a \pi_\theta(a|s')A^{\pi_\theta}(s',a)=0.
\]
This proves the claim.
\end{proof}

The identity \eqref{eq:softmax_grad} makes the coverage issue explicit:
the effective learning rate of coordinate $(s,a)$ is scaled by
$d_\mu^{\pi_\theta}(s)\pi_\theta(a|s)$.
If a state is rarely visited, then its parameters move slowly.

The vanilla policy gradient ascent update is
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:gd-softmax}
\theta^{(t+1)}=\theta^{(t)}+\eta \nabla_\theta V^{\pi_{\theta^{(t)}}}(\mu).
\end{equation}
We write
\[
\pi^{(t)}:=\pi_{\theta^{(t)}},
\qquad
V^{(t)}:=V^{\pi_{\theta^{(t)}}}.
\]

Although the problem is non-convex, the following theorem shows that the vanilla policy
gradient method is asymptotically globally convergent under a full-support assumption on $\mu$.

\begin{theorem}[Global convergence for the softmax parameterization]
\label{thm:glb-softmax}
Assume the update rule \eqref{eqn:gd-softmax} and suppose $\mu(s)>0$ for all
$s\in\Scal$. If
\[
\eta \le \frac{(1-\gamma)^3}{8},
\]
then for every state $s$,
\[
V^{(t)}(s)\to V^\star(s)
\qquad \text{as } t\to\infty.
\]
\end{theorem}

We do not prove this result here. The theorem is asymptotic, and the worst-case convergence
rate is believed to be extremely slow. The full-support condition on $\mu$ is essential:
without it, states outside the support of $d_\mu^{\pi_\theta}$ may receive vanishing updates
even when they are crucial for performance under another start-state distribution $\rho$.

\section{Log barrier regularization}

A major source of difficulty with softmax policies is that the parameters can grow large in
magnitude, driving the policy toward determinism and causing some action probabilities to become
extremely small. This slows learning and complicates global analysis.

To prevent this, we consider a barrier that penalizes small probabilities. Recall that
\[
\KL(p\|q)
:=
\E_{x\sim p}\!\left[\log \frac{p(x)}{q(x)}\right].
\]
Let $\mathrm{Unif}_{\Xcal}$ denote the uniform distribution over a finite set $\Xcal$.
Define the log-barrier regularized objective
\begin{align}
L_\lambda(\theta)
&:=
V^{\pi_\theta}(\mu)
-
\lambda \E_{s\sim \mathrm{Unif}_\Scal}
\Big[\KL(\mathrm{Unif}_\Acal \,\|\, \pi_\theta(\cdot|s))\Big] \nonumber\\
&=
V^{\pi_\theta}(\mu)
+
\frac{\lambda}{|\Scal||\Acal|}
\sum_{s,a} \log \pi_\theta(a|s)
+
\lambda \log |\Acal|.
\label{eqn:loss-reg}
\end{align}
The additive constant is irrelevant for optimization.

The regularized gradient ascent update is
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:gd-entropic}
\theta^{(t+1)}=\theta^{(t)}+\eta \nabla_\theta L_\lambda(\theta^{(t)}).
\end{equation}

The key fact is that approximate first-order stationarity for $L_\lambda$ implies
near-optimality for the original value function, with a dependence on the mismatch
between the optimization distribution $\mu$ and the target distribution $\rho$.

\begin{theorem}[Log barrier regularization]
\label{thm:small-gradient}
Suppose $\theta$ satisfies
\[
\|\nabla_\theta L_\lambda(\theta)\|_2 \le \varepsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{opt}} \le \frac{\lambda}{2|\Scal||\Acal|}.
\]
Then for every start-state distribution $\rho$,
\[
V^{\pi_\theta}(\rho)
\ge
V^\star(\rho)
-
\frac{2\lambda}{1-\gamma}
\left\|
\frac{d_\rho^{\pi^\star}}{\mu}
\right\|_\infty.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the performance difference lemma,
\begin{align*}
V^\star(\rho)-V^{\pi_\theta}(\rho)
&=
\frac{1}{1-\gamma}
\sum_{s,a} d_\rho^{\pi^\star}(s)\pi^\star(a|s)A^{\pi_\theta}(s,a) \\
&\le
\frac{1}{1-\gamma}
\sum_s d_\rho^{\pi^\star}(s)\max_{a} A^{\pi_\theta}(s,a).
\end{align*}
Thus it is enough to show that for every state $s$,
\[
\max_a A^{\pi_\theta}(s,a)\le \frac{2\lambda}{|\Scal|\mu(s)}.
\]

Fix $(s,a)$ such that $A^{\pi_\theta}(s,a)\ge 0$.
Differentiating \eqref{eqn:loss-reg} and using \eqref{eq:softmax_grad},
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:grad-KLobjective}
\frac{\partial L_\lambda(\theta)}{\partial \theta_{s,a}}
=
\frac{1}{1-\gamma} d_\mu^{\pi_\theta}(s)\pi_\theta(a|s)A^{\pi_\theta}(s,a)
+
\frac{\lambda}{|\Scal|}
\left(\frac{1}{|\Acal|}-\pi_\theta(a|s)\right).
\end{equation}
Since $A^{\pi_\theta}(s,a)\ge 0$, we have
\[
\frac{\partial L_\lambda(\theta)}{\partial \theta_{s,a}}
\ge
\frac{\lambda}{|\Scal|}
\left(\frac{1}{|\Acal|}-\pi_\theta(a|s)\right).
\]
Also,
\[
\left|
\frac{\partial L_\lambda(\theta)}{\partial \theta_{s,a}}
\right|
\le
\|\nabla_\theta L_\lambda(\theta)\|_2
\le
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{opt}},
\]
so
\[
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}
\ge
\frac{\lambda}{|\Scal|}
\left(\frac{1}{|\Acal|}-\pi_\theta(a|s)\right).
\]
Using $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{opt}} \le \lambda/(2|\Scal||\Acal|)$ gives
\[
\pi_\theta(a|s)\ge \frac{1}{2|\Acal|}.
\]

Now solve \eqref{eqn:grad-KLobjective} for the advantage:
\begin{align*}
A^{\pi_\theta}(s,a)
&=
\frac{1-\gamma}{d_\mu^{\pi_\theta}(s)\pi_\theta(a|s)}
\left(
\frac{\partial L_\lambda(\theta)}{\partial \theta_{s,a}}
-
\frac{\lambda}{|\Scal|}\left(\frac{1}{|\Acal|}-\pi_\theta(a|s)\right)
\right) \\
&=
\frac{1-\gamma}{d_\mu^{\pi_\theta}(s)}
\left(
\frac{1}{\pi_\theta(a|s)}\frac{\partial L_\lambda(\theta)}{\partial \theta_{s,a}}
+
\frac{\lambda}{|\Scal|}
\left(1-\frac{1}{\pi_\theta(a|s)|\Acal|}\right)
\right).
\end{align*}
Using $\pi_\theta(a|s)\ge 1/(2|\Acal|)$ and
$\left|\frac{\partial L_\lambda}{\partial \theta_{s,a}}\right|\le \varepsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}$,
\begin{align*}
A^{\pi_\theta}(s,a)
&\le
\frac{1-\gamma}{d_\mu^{\pi_\theta}(s)}
\left(
2|\Acal|\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}
+
\frac{\lambda}{|\Scal|}
\right) \\
&\le
\frac{2(1-\gamma)\lambda}{|\Scal|\,d_\mu^{\pi_\theta}(s)},
\end{align*}
where the last step uses $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}\le \lambda/(2|\Scal||\Acal|)$.
Finally, by \eqref{eq:dpi_lb_mu},
\[
d_\mu^{\pi_\theta}(s)\ge (1-\gamma)\mu(s),
\]
so
\[
A^{\pi_\theta}(s,a)\le \frac{2\lambda}{|\Scal|\mu(s)}.
\]
Since this holds for every $(s,a)$ with nonnegative advantage, the same bound holds for
$\max_a A^{\pi_\theta}(s,a)$. Substituting into the performance difference bound gives
\begin{align*}
V^\star(\rho)-V^{\pi_\theta}(\rho)
&\le
\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\sum_s d_\rho^{\pi^\star}(s)\frac{2\lambda}{|\Scal|\mu(s)} \\
&\le
\frac{2\lambda}{1-\gamma}
\left\|
\frac{d_\rho^{\pi^\star}}{\mu}
\right\|_\infty,
\end{align*}
since $\sum_s 1/|\Scal|=1$ after factoring out the maximum ratio.
\end{proof}

We refer to
\[
\left\|
\frac{d_\rho^{\pi^\star}}{\mu}
\right\|_\infty
\]
as the \emph{distribution mismatch coefficient}. The theorem shows that first-order stationarity
becomes globally meaningful once the barrier prevents vanishing action probabilities and the
optimization distribution $\mu$ provides adequate coverage of the states relevant for $\rho$.

Combining Theorem~\ref{thm:small-gradient} with Lemma~\ref{lemma:stationary} yields
an explicit iteration bound.

\begin{corollary}[Iteration complexity with log barrier regularization]
\label{corollary:entropy}
Assume the softmax policy class and suppose $L_\lambda$ is $\beta_\lambda$-smooth with
\[
\beta_\lambda
\le
\frac{8\gamma}{(1-\gamma)^3}+\frac{2\lambda}{|\Scal|}.
\]
Initialize at the uniform policy, e.g.\ $\theta^{(0)}=0$ in the tabular softmax parameterization,
and run the updates \eqref{eqn:gd-entropic} with step size $\eta=1/\beta_\lambda$.

Let
\[
\lambda
:=
\frac{\epsilon(1-\gamma)}
{2\left\|\frac{d_\rho^{\pi^\star}}{\mu}\right\|_\infty}.
\]
Then
\[
\min_{0\le t<T}\big\{V^\star(\rho)-V^{(t)}(\rho)\big\}\le \epsilon
\]
whenever
\[
T
\ge
\frac{320\,|\Scal|^2|\Acal|^2}{(1-\gamma)^6\,\epsilon^2}
\left\|
\frac{d_\rho^{\pi^\star}}{\mu}
\right\|_\infty^2.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:small-gradient}, it is enough to ensure
\[
\|\nabla_\theta L_\lambda(\theta^{(t)})\|_2 \le \frac{\lambda}{2|\Scal||\Acal|}.
\]
Since $\theta^{(0)}=0$ corresponds to the uniform policy, the regularization term vanishes initially,
so
\[
L_\lambda(\theta^{(0)})=V^{(0)}(\mu)\ge 0.
\]
Also $L_\lambda(\theta)\le V^{\pi_\theta}(\mu)\le 1/(1-\gamma)$ for every $\theta$, hence
\[
\sup_\theta L_\lambda(\theta)-L_\lambda(\theta^{(0)})
\le \frac{1}{1-\gamma}.
\]
Applying Lemma~\ref{lemma:stationary} to the objective $L_\lambda$ gives
\[
\min_{0\le t<T}\|\nabla_\theta L_\lambda(\theta^{(t)})\|_2^2
\le
\frac{2\beta_\lambda}{(1-\gamma)T}.
\]
Thus it suffices that
\[
\sqrt{\frac{2\beta_\lambda}{(1-\gamma)T}}
\le
\frac{\lambda}{2|\Scal||\Acal|},
\]
or equivalently,
\[
T\ge \frac{8\beta_\lambda |\Scal|^2|\Acal|^2}{(1-\gamma)\lambda^2}.
\]
Using the bound on $\beta_\lambda$,
\[
\frac{8\beta_\lambda |\Scal|^2|\Acal|^2}{(1-\gamma)\lambda^2}
\le
\frac{64|\Scal|^2|\Acal|^2}{(1-\gamma)^4\lambda^2}
+
\frac{16|\Scal||\Acal|^2}{(1-\gamma)\lambda}.
\]
Since $\left\|\frac{d_\rho^{\pi^\star}}{\mu}\right\|_\infty\ge 1$ and
$\epsilon\le 1/(1-\gamma)$ is the only meaningful regime, the chosen $\lambda$ satisfies
$\lambda\le 1$, so the second term is dominated by the first. Hence
\[
T
\ge
\frac{80|\Scal|^2|\Acal|^2}{(1-\gamma)^4\lambda^2}
\]
is sufficient. Substituting the chosen $\lambda$ yields the stated bound.
\end{proof}

The corollary gives a polynomial iteration complexity bound, but it depends on the
distribution mismatch coefficient and on a strong barrier that keeps the policy away
from the boundary of the simplex.

It is worth contrasting this log barrier with the more common entropy regularizer
\[
\frac{1}{|\Scal|}\sum_s H(\pi_\theta(\cdot|s))
=
\frac{1}{|\Scal|}\sum_s \sum_a -\pi_\theta(a|s)\log \pi_\theta(a|s).
\]
The entropy regularizer is bounded, whereas the barrier
\[
\KL(\mathrm{Unif}_\Acal\|\pi_\theta(\cdot|s))
\]
diverges as any action probability tends to zero. The proof above crucially uses this
aggressive penalty against vanishing probabilities.

\section{The natural policy gradient}

We now turn to a preconditioned gradient method based on the information geometry
of the policy class.

For a parametric density $p_\theta(x)$, the Fisher information matrix is
\[
\E_{x\sim p_\theta}
\big[
\nabla_\theta \log p_\theta(x)\,\nabla_\theta \log p_\theta(x)^\top
\big].
\]
For policies, we average this quantity over the discounted state-visitation distribution:
\[
F_\rho(\theta)
:=
\E_{s\sim d_\rho^{\pi_\theta}}
\E_{a\sim \pi_\theta(\cdot|s)}
\Big[
\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a|s)\,
\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a|s)^\top
\Big].
\]
The natural policy gradient (NPG) update is
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:npg}
\theta^{(t+1)}
=
\theta^{(t)} + \eta\,F_\rho(\theta^{(t)})^\dagger \nabla_\theta V^{(t)}(\rho),
\end{equation}
where $\dagger$ denotes the Moore--Penrose pseudoinverse.

In this section we optimize directly with respect to the performance distribution $\rho$,
so there is no separate optimization measure $\mu$.

For the softmax parameterization, the NPG update takes a particularly simple form.

\begin{lemma}[Softmax NPG as soft policy iteration]
\label{lemma:npg-softmax}
For the softmax parameterization \eqref{eq:softmax}, the NPG updates \eqref{eqn:npg} satisfy
\[
\theta^{(t+1)}
=
\theta^{(t)} + \frac{\eta}{1-\gamma}A^{(t)} + \eta v,
\]
where $v$ is a state-dependent offset, i.e.\ $v_{s,a}=c_s$ for some constants $\{c_s\}_{s\in\Scal}$.
Consequently,
\[
\pi^{(t+1)}(a|s)
=
\pi^{(t)}(a|s)\,
\frac{\exp\!\big(\eta A^{(t)}(s,a)/(1-\gamma)\big)}{Z_t(s)},
\]
where
\[
Z_t(s):=\sum_{a\in\Acal}\pi^{(t)}(a|s)\exp\!\big(\eta A^{(t)}(s,a)/(1-\gamma)\big).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $\pi=\pi_\theta$ for brevity. By Lemma~\ref{eq:softmax-grad},
\[
\big[\nabla_\theta V^\pi(\rho)\big]_{s,a}
=
\frac{1}{1-\gamma} d_\rho^\pi(s)\pi(a|s)A^\pi(s,a).
\]
For any vector $w\in\R^{|\Scal||\Acal|}$, define
\[
\bar w_s := \sum_{a\in\Acal}\pi(a|s)w_{s,a}.
\]
Using the softmax derivative,
\[
w^\top \nabla_\theta \log \pi(a|s)=w_{s,a}-\bar w_s.
\]
Hence
\[
[F_\rho(\theta)w]_{s,a}
=
d_\rho^\pi(s)\pi(a|s)\big(w_{s,a}-\bar w_s\big).
\]
Comparing with the gradient formula above shows that any solution of
\[
F_\rho(\theta)w=\nabla_\theta V^\pi(\rho)
\]
must satisfy
\[
w_{s,a}-\bar w_s = \frac{1}{1-\gamma}A^\pi(s,a).
\]
Equivalently, every solution is of the form
\[
w=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}A^\pi+v,
\]
where $v$ is state-dependent: $v_{s,a}=c_s$.
The pseudoinverse selects the minimum-norm solution among these.

Substituting this into the update of $\theta$ proves the first claim.
Since the softmax policy is invariant to adding the same constant to all action logits at
a state, the state-dependent offset $v$ disappears in the induced policy update, yielding
the multiplicative-weights form.
\end{proof}

A notable feature of this formula is that, although the ascent direction in
\eqref{eqn:npg} is defined using the gradient of $V^\pi(\rho)$, the closed-form update
for the softmax policy depends only on the advantages and not explicitly on the visitation
distribution $d_\rho^\pi$.

The following improvement lemma is the key quantitative step.

\begin{lemma}[Improvement lower bound for NPG]
\label{lemma:npg-gap}
For the iterates generated by \eqref{eqn:npg}, for every start-state distribution $\mu$,
\[
V^{(t+1)}(\mu)-V^{(t)}(\mu)
\ge
\frac{1-\gamma}{\eta}\,
\E_{s\sim \mu}\big[\log Z_t(s)\big]
\ge 0.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
First, Jensen's inequality and the identity
\[
\E_{a\sim \pi^{(t)}(\cdot|s)}[A^{(t)}(s,a)]=0
\]
imply
\begin{align*}
\log Z_t(s)
&=
\log \sum_a \pi^{(t)}(a|s)\exp\!\big(\eta A^{(t)}(s,a)/(1-\gamma)\big) \\
&\ge
\sum_a \pi^{(t)}(a|s)\frac{\eta}{1-\gamma}A^{(t)}(s,a)
=
0.
\end{align*}

By the performance difference lemma,
\[
V^{(t+1)}(\mu)-V^{(t)}(\mu)
=
\frac{1}{1-\gamma}
\E_{s\sim d_\mu^{(t+1)}}
\E_{a\sim \pi^{(t+1)}(\cdot|s)}
\big[A^{(t)}(s,a)\big].
\]
Using the update formula from Lemma~\ref{lemma:npg-softmax},
\[
\frac{\eta}{1-\gamma}A^{(t)}(s,a)
=
\log \frac{\pi^{(t+1)}(a|s)Z_t(s)}{\pi^{(t)}(a|s)}.
\]
Therefore
\begin{align*}
V^{(t+1)}(\mu)-V^{(t)}(\mu)
&=
\frac{1}{\eta}
\E_{s\sim d_\mu^{(t+1)}}
\E_{a\sim \pi^{(t+1)}(\cdot|s)}
\left[
\log \frac{\pi^{(t+1)}(a|s)Z_t(s)}{\pi^{(t)}(a|s)}
\right] \\
&=
\frac{1}{\eta}
\E_{s\sim d_\mu^{(t+1)}}
\left[
\KL(\pi_s^{(t+1)}\|\pi_s^{(t)})
+
\log Z_t(s)
\right] \\
&\ge
\frac{1}{\eta}
\E_{s\sim d_\mu^{(t+1)}}[\log Z_t(s)].
\end{align*}
Since $\log Z_t(s)\ge 0$ and $d_\mu^{(t+1)}(s)\ge (1-\gamma)\mu(s)$ by \eqref{eq:dpi_lb_mu},
\[
\E_{s\sim d_\mu^{(t+1)}}[\log Z_t(s)]
\ge
(1-\gamma)\E_{s\sim \mu}[\log Z_t(s)].
\]
This proves the claim.
\end{proof}

We can now show that NPG enjoys a dimension-free global convergence rate.

\begin{theorem}[Global convergence for NPG]
\label{thm:npg}
Suppose we run the NPG updates \eqref{eqn:npg} with start distribution $\rho$,
initialization $\theta^{(0)}=0$, and stepsize $\eta>0$.
Then for every $T\ge 1$,
\[
V^{(T)}(\rho)
\ge
V^\star(\rho)
-
\frac{\log |\Acal|}{\eta T}
-
\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)^2 T}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let
\[
d^\star := d_\rho^{\pi^\star}.
\]
Since $\theta^{(0)}=0$, the initial policy is uniform at every state, so
\[
\KL(\pi_s^\star\|\pi_s^{(0)})\le \log |\Acal|
\qquad \forall s\in\Scal.
\]

By the performance difference lemma,
\begin{align*}
V^\star(\rho)-V^{(t)}(\rho)
&=
\frac{1}{1-\gamma}
\E_{s\sim d^\star}
\E_{a\sim \pi^\star(\cdot|s)}[A^{(t)}(s,a)].
\end{align*}
Using the update formula from Lemma~\ref{lemma:npg-softmax},
\[
\frac{\eta}{1-\gamma}A^{(t)}(s,a)
=
\log \frac{\pi^{(t+1)}(a|s)Z_t(s)}{\pi^{(t)}(a|s)}.
\]
Hence
\begin{align*}
V^\star(\rho)-V^{(t)}(\rho)
&=
\frac{1}{\eta}
\E_{s\sim d^\star}
\E_{a\sim \pi^\star(\cdot|s)}
\left[
\log \frac{\pi^{(t+1)}(a|s)Z_t(s)}{\pi^{(t)}(a|s)}
\right] \\
&=
\frac{1}{\eta}
\E_{s\sim d^\star}
\left[
\KL(\pi_s^\star\|\pi_s^{(t)})
-
\KL(\pi_s^\star\|\pi_s^{(t+1)})
+
\log Z_t(s)
\right].
\end{align*}

Next, apply Lemma~\ref{lemma:npg-gap} with start distribution $d^\star$:
\[
\frac{1}{\eta}\E_{s\sim d^\star}[\log Z_t(s)]
\le
\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\big(V^{(t+1)}(d^\star)-V^{(t)}(d^\star)\big).
\]
Therefore
\begin{align*}
V^\star(\rho)-V^{(t)}(\rho)
&\le
\frac{1}{\eta}
\E_{s\sim d^\star}
\big[
\KL(\pi_s^\star\|\pi_s^{(t)})
-
\KL(\pi_s^\star\|\pi_s^{(t+1)})
\big] \\
&\qquad +
\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\big(V^{(t+1)}(d^\star)-V^{(t)}(d^\star)\big).
\end{align*}

Now average over $t=0,\ldots,T-1$.
Since Lemma~\ref{lemma:npg-gap} implies $V^{(t+1)}(\rho)\ge V^{(t)}(\rho)$, we have
\[
V^\star(\rho)-V^{(T)}(\rho)
\le
\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\big(V^\star(\rho)-V^{(t)}(\rho)\big).
\]
Using the previous display and telescoping,
\begin{align*}
V^\star(\rho)-V^{(T)}(\rho)
&\le
\frac{1}{\eta T}
\E_{s\sim d^\star}\KL(\pi_s^\star\|\pi_s^{(0)})
+
\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)T}\big(V^{(T)}(d^\star)-V^{(0)}(d^\star)\big) \\
&\le
\frac{\log |\Acal|}{\eta T} + \frac{1}{(1-\gamma)^2T},
\end{align*}
since values lie in $[0,1/(1-\gamma)]$.
\end{proof}

Thus, by choosing
\[
\eta \ge (1-\gamma)^2 \log |\Acal|,
\]
we obtain
\[
V^{(T)}(\rho)\ge V^\star(\rho)-\frac{2}{(1-\gamma)^2T}.
\]
In particular, NPG reaches an $\epsilon$-optimal policy after
\[
T \le \frac{2}{(1-\gamma)^2\epsilon}
\]
iterations, with no explicit dependence on $|\Scal|$.

\section{Bibliographic Remarks and Further Readings}\label{bib:pg_opt}

The natural policy gradient method was introduced in \cite{Kakade01}; see also
\cite{bagnell2003covariant,Peters:2008:NA:1352927.1352986} for information-geometric
perspectives.

The convergence results in this chapter are largely based on \cite{agarwal2019optimality}.
The proof strategy behind the NPG rate has connections to online-learning analyses in changing
MDPs \cite{even-dar2009online}; the sharper $O(1/T)$ rate stated here is due to
\cite{agarwal2019optimality}. Closely related $O(1/T)$ guarantees also follow from the analysis of
mirror-descent modified policy iteration in \cite{geist2019theory}. The observation that the
online-learning proof strategy yields a convergence guarantee for NPG was made in
\cite{DBLP:journals/corr/NeuJG17}.

Building on \cite{agarwal2019optimality}, \cite{mei2020global} showed that both vanilla policy
gradient ascent and entropy-regularized policy gradient ascent for the softmax class converge
asymptotically at a $O(1/t)$ rate. It is not expected that these methods admit finite-time
polynomial guarantees in the relevant structural quantities (such as $|\Scal|$, $|\Acal|$,
$1/(1-\gamma)$, and the distribution mismatch coefficient).

Entropy regularization is standard in practical policy-gradient methods; see
\cite{mnih2016asynchronous} and the more detailed empirical discussion in \cite{ahmed2019understanding}.
