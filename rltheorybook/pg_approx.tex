\chapter{Function Approximation and the Natural Policy Gradient}
\label{chap:pg_approx}

In Chapter~\ref{bib:pg_opt}, we analyzed the natural policy gradient in the tabular softmax
policy class and obtained global convergence guarantees. We now turn to the setting of
\emph{function approximation}, where the policy class
\[
\Pi=\{\pi_\theta:\theta\in\R^d\}
\]
may be far smaller than the set of all stochastic policies. In particular, we no longer
assume that $\Pi$ contains an optimal policy.

Accordingly, we adopt an \emph{agnostic} viewpoint: given a start-state distribution
$\rho$, we seek guarantees relative to a comparator policy $\widetilde \pi$ (often the
best policy in the class, but not necessarily). As in the previous chapter, it will be
useful to distinguish the distribution with respect to which performance is measured from
the distribution under which gradients or regression problems are formed.

Our focus is on the natural policy gradient (NPG) update
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:vanilla_NPG}
\theta \leftarrow \theta + \eta\, F_\rho(\theta)^\dagger \nabla_\theta V^{\pi_\theta}(\rho),
\end{equation}
and on a closely related variant, which we call \emph{$Q$-NPG}. The key theme of the
chapter is that the effectiveness of these updates is governed by a precise
approximation--estimation decomposition based on \emph{compatible function approximation}.

\section{Compatible function approximation and the NPG}

We begin by recalling the geometric meaning of the natural policy gradient.

Fix a start-state distribution $\mu$ and a parameter $\theta$. Let $\pi=\pi_\theta$ for
brevity, and define the Fisher matrix
\[
F_\mu(\theta)
:=
\E_{s\sim d_\mu^\pi}
\E_{a\sim \pi(\cdot|s)}
\Big[
\nabla_\theta \log \pi(a|s)\,\nabla_\theta \log \pi(a|s)^\top
\Big].
\]

The compatible function approximation problem is the regression problem of approximating
the advantage function using the score features $\nabla_\theta \log \pi(a|s)$.

\begin{lemma}[Compatible function approximation]
\label{lemma:compatible}
Fix $\mu$ and $\theta$, and let $\pi=\pi_\theta$.
Let $w^\star$ be any minimizer of
\[
\min_{w\in\R^d}
\E_{s\sim d_\mu^\pi}
\E_{a\sim \pi(\cdot|s)}
\Big[
\big(A^\pi(s,a)-w^\top \nabla_\theta \log \pi(a|s)\big)^2
\Big].
\]
Define the predictor
\[
\widehat A^\pi(s,a):=(w^\star)^\top \nabla_\theta \log \pi(a|s).
\]
Then
\[
\nabla_\theta V^\pi(\mu)
=
\frac{1}{1-\gamma}
\E_{s\sim d_\mu^\pi}
\E_{a\sim \pi(\cdot|s)}
\Big[
\nabla_\theta \log \pi(a|s)\,\widehat A^\pi(s,a)
\Big].
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The first-order optimality condition for $w^\star$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:first_order_compat}
\E_{s\sim d_\mu^\pi}
\E_{a\sim \pi(\cdot|s)}
\Big[
\big(A^\pi(s,a)-(w^\star)^\top \nabla_\theta \log \pi(a|s)\big)
\nabla_\theta \log \pi(a|s)
\Big]
=
0.
\end{equation}
Rearranging and using the advantage form of the policy gradient from
Theorem~\ref{thm:pg_expressions} yields
\[
\nabla_\theta V^\pi(\mu)
=
\frac{1}{1-\gamma}
\E_{s\sim d_\mu^\pi}
\E_{a\sim \pi(\cdot|s)}
\Big[
\nabla_\theta \log \pi(a|s)\,\widehat A^\pi(s,a)
\Big].
\]
\end{proof}

The next lemma shows that the NPG update direction is exactly the solution of this
regression problem.

\begin{lemma}\label{eq:npg_argmin}
Fix $\mu$ and $\theta$, and let $\pi=\pi_\theta$.
Let $w^\star$ denote the minimum-Euclidean-norm minimizer of
\[
\min_{w\in\R^d}
\E_{s\sim d_\mu^\pi}
\E_{a\sim \pi(\cdot|s)}
\Big[
\big(A^\pi(s,a)-w^\top \nabla_\theta \log \pi(a|s)\big)^2
\Big].
\]
Then
\[
F_\mu(\theta)^\dagger \nabla_\theta V^\pi(\mu)
=
\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\, w^\star.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:first_order_compat},
\[
\E_{s\sim d_\mu^\pi}
\E_{a\sim \pi(\cdot|s)}
\Big[
\nabla_\theta \log \pi(a|s)\,A^\pi(s,a)
\Big]
=
F_\mu(\theta)\,w^\star.
\]
Using the advantage expression from Theorem~\ref{thm:pg_expressions},
\[
\nabla_\theta V^\pi(\mu)
=
\frac{1}{1-\gamma}F_\mu(\theta)\,w^\star.
\]
Thus $w^\star$ is a minimum-norm solution of the linear system
\[
F_\mu(\theta)w=(1-\gamma)\nabla_\theta V^\pi(\mu),
\]
which is exactly the Moore--Penrose pseudoinverse solution.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{eq:npg_argmin} shows that the NPG update can be viewed as
\[
\theta \leftarrow \theta + \frac{\eta}{1-\gamma} w^\star,
\]
where $w^\star$ is the compatible regression solution. This viewpoint is central in what
follows.

\section{Examples: NPG and $Q$-NPG}
\label{section:examples}

We now specialize the compatible approximation viewpoint to the policy classes most often
used in practice. Throughout this section, we consider policy classes of the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:policy_class}
\Pi
=
\left\{
\pi_\theta(a|s)
=
\frac{\exp(f_\theta(s,a))}
{\sum_{a'\in\Acal}\exp(f_\theta(s,a'))}
\;\middle|\;
\theta\in\R^d
\right\},
\end{equation}
where $f_\theta(s,a)$ is differentiable.

\subsection{Log-linear policy classes}
\label{section:log_linear}

Suppose $f_\theta(s,a)=\theta^\top \phi_{s,a}$ for features $\phi_{s,a}\in\R^d$.
Then
\[
\pi_\theta(a|s)
=
\frac{\exp(\theta^\top \phi_{s,a})}
{\sum_{a'\in\Acal}\exp(\theta^\top \phi_{s,a'})}.
\]
Define the centered features
\[
\bar \phi_{s,a}^{\,\pi}
:=
\phi_{s,a}-\E_{a'\sim \pi(\cdot|s)}[\phi_{s,a'}].
\]
A direct calculation shows
\[
\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a|s)=\bar \phi_{s,a}^{\,\pi_\theta}.
\]

Hence, by Lemma~\ref{eq:npg_argmin}, the NPG update
\eqref{eqn:vanilla_NPG} is equivalent, up to rescaling of the learning rate, to
\[
\theta \leftarrow \theta + \eta w^\star,
\qquad
w^\star \in \argmin_w
\E_{s\sim d_\rho^{\pi_\theta},\,a\sim \pi_\theta(\cdot|s)}
\Big[
\big(A^{\pi_\theta}(s,a)-w^\top \bar \phi_{s,a}^{\,\pi_\theta}\big)^2
\Big].
\]

We will also consider a variant based on regressing $Q$ rather than $A$:
\[
\theta \leftarrow \theta + \eta w^\star,
\qquad
w^\star \in \argmin_w
\E_{s\sim d_\rho^{\pi_\theta},\,a\sim \pi_\theta(\cdot|s)}
\Big[
\big(Q^{\pi_\theta}(s,a)-w^\top \phi_{s,a}\big)^2
\Big].
\]
We refer to this update rule as \emph{$Q$-NPG}.

For log-linear policies, these updates can also be written directly in policy space.
Indeed, an update of the form $\theta \leftarrow \theta+\eta w$ induces
\[
\pi(a|s)\ \leftarrow\ \frac{\pi(a|s)\exp(\eta\, w^\top \phi_{s,a})}{Z_s},
\]
where $Z_s$ is the normalization constant. In the NPG case one may equivalently replace
$\phi_{s,a}$ by the centered feature $\bar\phi_{s,a}^{\,\pi}$ inside the exponential,
since the difference is a state-dependent shift that is absorbed by normalization.

\subsection{Neural policy classes}
\label{sec:neural}

Now let $f_\theta(s,a)$ be a differentiable neural network.
Define
\[
g_\theta(s,a)
:=
\nabla_\theta f_\theta(s,a)
-
\E_{a'\sim \pi_\theta(\cdot|s)}[\nabla_\theta f_\theta(s,a')].
\]
Then
\[
\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a|s)=g_\theta(s,a).
\]
Accordingly, the NPG update takes the form
\[
\theta \leftarrow \theta + \eta w^\star,
\qquad
w^\star \in \argmin_w
\E_{s\sim d_\rho^{\pi_\theta},\,a\sim \pi_\theta(\cdot|s)}
\Big[
\big(A^{\pi_\theta}(s,a)-w^\top g_\theta(s,a)\big)^2
\Big].
\]
The $Q$-NPG variant is
\[
\theta \leftarrow \theta + \eta w^\star,
\qquad
w^\star \in \argmin_w
\E_{s\sim d_\rho^{\pi_\theta},\,a\sim \pi_\theta(\cdot|s)}
\Big[
\big(Q^{\pi_\theta}(s,a)-w^\top \nabla_\theta f_\theta(s,a)\big)^2
\Big].
\]

\section{A regret lemma for approximate NPG updates}

We now give a generic mirror-descent-style inequality for updates of the form
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:general_update}
\theta^{(t+1)}=\theta^{(t)}+\eta w^{(t)}.
\end{equation}
The sequence $w^{(0)},\ldots,w^{(T-1)}$ is arbitrary; later we will specialize it to
approximate regression solutions.

Let $\widetilde \pi$ be an arbitrary comparator policy and write
\[
\widetilde d := d_\rho^{\widetilde \pi}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[NPG regret lemma]
\label{lemma:npg_regret}
Assume that for every state $s$ and action $a$, the map
\[
\theta \mapsto \log \pi_\theta(a|s)
\]
is $\beta$-smooth. Consider the update rule \eqref{eqn:general_update}, initialized at the
uniform policy $\pi^{(0)}(\cdot|s)=\mathrm{Unif}_\Acal(\cdot)$ for all $s$, and assume
\[
\|w^{(t)}\|_2 \le W
\qquad \text{for all } t<T.
\]
Define
\[
\mathrm{err}_t
:=
\E_{s\sim \widetilde d}
\E_{a\sim \widetilde \pi(\cdot|s)}
\Big[
A^{(t)}(s,a)- (w^{(t)})^\top \nabla_\theta \log \pi^{(t)}(a|s)
\Big].
\]
If
\[
\eta
=
\sqrt{\frac{2\log |\Acal|}{\beta W^2 T}},
\]
then
\[
\min_{0\le t<T}\big\{V^{\widetilde \pi}(\rho)-V^{(t)}(\rho)\big\}
\le
\frac{1}{1-\gamma}
\left(
W\sqrt{\frac{2\beta \log |\Acal|}{T}}
+
\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathrm{err}_t
\right).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By smoothness of $\log \pi_\theta(a|s)$,
\[
\log \pi^{(t+1)}(a|s)-\log \pi^{(t)}(a|s)
\ge
\eta \nabla_\theta \log \pi^{(t)}(a|s)^\top w^{(t)}
-
\frac{\beta \eta^2}{2}\|w^{(t)}\|_2^2.
\]
Taking expectation over $s\sim \widetilde d$, $a\sim \widetilde\pi(\cdot|s)$ gives
\begin{align*}
&\E_{s\sim \widetilde d}
\Big[
\KL(\widetilde\pi_s\|\pi^{(t)}_s)-\KL(\widetilde\pi_s\|\pi^{(t+1)}_s)
\Big] \\
&\qquad \ge
\eta\,
\E_{s\sim \widetilde d}
\E_{a\sim \widetilde\pi(\cdot|s)}
\Big[
\nabla_\theta \log \pi^{(t)}(a|s)^\top w^{(t)}
\Big]
-
\frac{\beta\eta^2}{2}W^2.
\end{align*}
Add and subtract $A^{(t)}(s,a)$ inside the expectation, and use the performance
difference lemma:
\[
\E_{s\sim \widetilde d}
\E_{a\sim \widetilde\pi(\cdot|s)}
[A^{(t)}(s,a)]
=
(1-\gamma)\big(V^{\widetilde \pi}(\rho)-V^{(t)}(\rho)\big).
\]
This yields
\[
(1-\gamma)\eta\big(V^{\widetilde \pi}(\rho)-V^{(t)}(\rho)\big)
\le
\E_{s\sim \widetilde d}
\Big[
\KL(\widetilde\pi_s\|\pi^{(t)}_s)-\KL(\widetilde\pi_s\|\pi^{(t+1)}_s)
\Big]
+
\frac{\beta\eta^2}{2}W^2
+
\eta\,\mathrm{err}_t.
\]
Summing over $t=0,\dots,T-1$ and telescoping,
\begin{align*}
\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\big(V^{\widetilde \pi}(\rho)-V^{(t)}(\rho)\big)
&\le
\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)\eta T}
\E_{s\sim \widetilde d}\KL(\widetilde\pi_s\|\pi^{(0)}_s) \\
&\qquad +
\frac{\eta\beta W^2}{2(1-\gamma)}
+
\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathrm{err}_t.
\end{align*}
Since $\pi^{(0)}_s$ is uniform, $\KL(\widetilde\pi_s\|\pi^{(0)}_s)\le \log |\Acal|$ for every $s$.
Substituting the chosen $\eta$ gives the claim.
\end{proof}

When $\mathrm{err}_t=0$ for all $t$, the lemma yields an $O(T^{-1/2})$ rate.
This is slower than the sharper $O(1/T)$ rate obtained in Theorem~\ref{thm:npg}
for exact NPG in the tabular softmax class. The point of
Lemma~\ref{lemma:npg_regret} is not sharpness, but robustness:
it cleanly isolates the effect of approximation and estimation error.

\section{$Q$-NPG with function approximation}
\label{sec:q-npg}

We now specialize to the log-linear policy class from Section~\ref{section:log_linear} and
analyze an approximate $Q$-NPG update.

For a state-action distribution $\upsilon$, define the squared loss
\[
L(w;\theta,\upsilon)
:=
\E_{(s,a)\sim \upsilon}
\Big[
\big(Q^{\pi_\theta}(s,a)-w^\top \phi_{s,a}\big)^2
\Big].
\]

To discuss coverage, we allow the update to be based on a state-action distribution
$\nu$ rather than only a state distribution. For a starting pair $(s_0,a_0)$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:c_def}
d_{s_0,a_0}^\pi(s,a)
:=
(1-\gamma)\sum_{t=0}^\infty \gamma^t
\Pr^\pi(s_t=s,a_t=a\mid s_0,a_0).
\end{equation}
For a distribution $\nu$ over $\Scal\times\Acal$, define
\[
d_\nu^\pi(s,a)
:=
\E_{(s_0,a_0)\sim \nu}\big[d_{s_0,a_0}^\pi(s,a)\big].
\]
We abbreviate
\[
d^{(t)}:=d_\nu^{\pi^{(t)}}.
\]

The approximate $Q$-NPG update is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:q-npg}
\theta^{(t+1)}
=
\theta^{(t)}+\eta w^{(t)},
\qquad
w^{(t)}\in \Wcal:=\{w:\|w\|_2\le W\},
\end{equation}
where $w^{(t)}$ is intended to approximately minimize
$L(w;\theta^{(t)},d^{(t)})$ over $\Wcal$.
We denote the exact constrained minimizer by
\[
w_\star^{(t)}\in \argmin_{w\in \Wcal} L(w;\theta^{(t)},d^{(t)}).
\]

We now separate \emph{excess risk} from \emph{approximation error}.

\begin{assumption}[Approximation and estimation error]
\label{assum:approx_est}
For all $t<T$, the iterates satisfy
\[
L(w^{(t)};\theta^{(t)},d^{(t)})
-
L(w_\star^{(t)};\theta^{(t)},d^{(t)})
\le \epsstat
\]
and
\[
L(w_\star^{(t)};\theta^{(t)},d^{(t)})\le \epsapprox.
\]
\end{assumption}

The estimation term $\epsstat$ reflects how accurately the regression problem is solved.
The approximation term $\epsapprox$ reflects model misspecification: even the best linear
predictor of $Q^{\pi^{(t)}}$ under the fitting distribution $d^{(t)}$ may not be exact.

We also need a coverage assumption on the feature covariance. For any state-action distribution
$\upsilon$, let
\[
\Sigma_\upsilon := \E_{(s,a)\sim \upsilon}\big[\phi_{s,a}\phi_{s,a}^\top\big].
\]

Fix a comparator policy $\pi^\star$ and define the associated state-action distribution
\[
d^\star(s,a)
:=
d_\rho^{\pi^\star}(s)\cdot \mathrm{Unif}_\Acal(a).
\]

\begin{assumption}[Relative condition number]
\label{assum:conditioning}
The quantity
\[
\kappa
:=
\sup_{w\in\R^d\setminus\{0\}}
\frac{w^\top \Sigma_{d^\star}w}{w^\top \Sigma_\nu w}
\]
is finite.
\end{assumption}

Thus $\nu$ covers all feature directions that matter under the comparator distribution
$d^\star$. As in linear bandits and linearly parameterized MDPs, this is a feature-space
coverage assumption rather than a state-space one.

We can now state the main result.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:q_npg_fa}
Fix a start-state distribution $\rho$, a start state-action distribution $\nu$, and an
arbitrary comparator policy $\pi^\star$.
Assume the log-linear policy class, Assumptions~\ref{assum:approx_est} and
\ref{assum:conditioning}, and that $\|\phi_{s,a}\|_2\le B$ for all $(s,a)$.
Suppose $\theta^{(0)}=0$ and
\[
\eta
=
\sqrt{\frac{2\log |\Acal|}{B^2W^2T}}.
\]
Then
\begin{align*}
\E\Big[\min_{0\le t<T}\big\{V^{\pi^\star}(\rho)-V^{(t)}(\rho)\big\}\Big]
&\le
\frac{BW}{1-\gamma}\sqrt{\frac{2\log |\Acal|}{T}} \\
&\quad +
\frac{2}{1-\gamma}
\sqrt{\frac{|\Acal|}{1-\gamma}
\left\|\frac{d^\star}{\nu}\right\|_\infty \epsapprox} \\
&\quad +
\frac{2}{1-\gamma}
\sqrt{\frac{|\Acal|\kappa}{1-\gamma}\,\epsstat}.
\end{align*}
\end{theorem}

The theorem makes the distinction between approximation and estimation especially clear.
The approximation error is transferred to the comparator distribution through the mismatch
factor $\|d^\star/\nu\|_\infty$, while the estimation term depends only on the relative
conditioning parameter $\kappa$.

\begin{proof}
For the log-linear policy class, $\log \pi_\theta(a|s)$ is $B^2$-smooth as a function of
$\theta$. Applying Lemma~\ref{lemma:npg_regret} with comparator $\widetilde\pi=\pi^\star$,
\[
\min_{0\le t<T}\big\{V^{\pi^\star}(\rho)-V^{(t)}(\rho)\big\}
\le
\frac{BW}{1-\gamma}\sqrt{\frac{2\log |\Acal|}{T}}
+
\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathrm{err}_t,
\]
where
\[
\mathrm{err}_t
=
\E_{s\sim d_\rho^{\pi^\star}}
\E_{a\sim \pi^\star(\cdot|s)}
\Big[
A^{(t)}(s,a)-(w^{(t)})^\top \bar \phi_{s,a}^{\,\pi^{(t)}}
\Big].
\]

We first decompose
\[
\mathrm{err}_t
=
T_{1,t}+T_{2,t},
\]
where
\begin{align*}
T_{1,t}
&:=
\E_{s\sim d_\rho^{\pi^\star}}
\E_{a\sim \pi^\star(\cdot|s)}
\Big[
A^{(t)}(s,a)-(w_\star^{(t)})^\top \bar \phi_{s,a}^{\,\pi^{(t)}}
\Big], \\
T_{2,t}
&:=
\E_{s\sim d_\rho^{\pi^\star}}
\E_{a\sim \pi^\star(\cdot|s)}
\Big[
(w_\star^{(t)}-w^{(t)})^\top \bar \phi_{s,a}^{\,\pi^{(t)}}
\Big].
\end{align*}

For the first term, write
\[
e_t(s,a)
:=
Q^{(t)}(s,a)-(w_\star^{(t)})^\top \phi_{s,a}.
\]
Since
\[
A^{(t)}(s,a)-(w_\star^{(t)})^\top \bar \phi_{s,a}^{\,\pi^{(t)}}
=
e_t(s,a)-\E_{a'\sim \pi^{(t)}(\cdot|s)}[e_t(s,a')],
\]
we have
\begin{align*}
|T_{1,t}|
&\le
\E_{s\sim d_\rho^{\pi^\star}}
\E_{a\sim \pi^\star(\cdot|s)}[|e_t(s,a)|]
+
\E_{s\sim d_\rho^{\pi^\star}}
\E_{a\sim \pi^{(t)}(\cdot|s)}[|e_t(s,a)|] \\
&\le
\sqrt{\E_{s\sim d_\rho^{\pi^\star},\,a\sim \pi^\star(\cdot|s)}[e_t(s,a)^2]}
+
\sqrt{\E_{s\sim d_\rho^{\pi^\star},\,a\sim \pi^{(t)}(\cdot|s)}[e_t(s,a)^2]}.
\end{align*}
By the definition of $d^\star(s,a)=d_\rho^{\pi^\star}(s)\mathrm{Unif}_\Acal(a)$,
for any conditional action distribution $\alpha(\cdot|s)$,
\[
\E_{s\sim d_\rho^{\pi^\star},\,a\sim \alpha(\cdot|s)}[e_t(s,a)^2]
\le
|\Acal|\,\E_{(s,a)\sim d^\star}[e_t(s,a)^2].
\]
Therefore,
\[
|T_{1,t}|
\le
2\sqrt{|\Acal|\,L(w_\star^{(t)};\theta^{(t)},d^\star)}.
\]
Next,
\[
L(w_\star^{(t)};\theta^{(t)},d^\star)
\le
\left\|\frac{d^\star}{d^{(t)}}\right\|_\infty
L(w_\star^{(t)};\theta^{(t)},d^{(t)}).
\]
Since $d^{(t)}=d_\nu^{\pi^{(t)}}$ and \eqref{eqn:c_def} implies
\[
d^{(t)}(s,a)\ge (1-\gamma)\nu(s,a),
\]
we have
\[
\left\|\frac{d^\star}{d^{(t)}}\right\|_\infty
\le
\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\left\|\frac{d^\star}{\nu}\right\|_\infty.
\]
Using Assumption~\ref{assum:approx_est},
\[
|T_{1,t}|
\le
2\sqrt{\frac{|\Acal|}{1-\gamma}\left\|\frac{d^\star}{\nu}\right\|_\infty \epsapprox}.
\]

For the second term, let
\[
u_t := w_\star^{(t)}-w^{(t)}.
\]
Since
\[
u_t^\top \bar\phi_{s,a}^{\,\pi^{(t)}}
=
u_t^\top \phi_{s,a}
-
\E_{a'\sim \pi^{(t)}(\cdot|s)}[u_t^\top \phi_{s,a'}],
\]
the same argument as above gives
\[
|T_{2,t}|
\le
2\sqrt{|\Acal|\,u_t^\top \Sigma_{d^\star}u_t}.
\]
By Assumption~\ref{assum:conditioning},
\[
u_t^\top \Sigma_{d^\star}u_t
\le
\kappa\,u_t^\top \Sigma_\nu u_t.
\]
Again using $d^{(t)}\ge (1-\gamma)\nu$,
\[
u_t^\top \Sigma_\nu u_t
\le
\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\,u_t^\top \Sigma_{d^{(t)}}u_t.
\]
It remains to control $u_t^\top \Sigma_{d^{(t)}}u_t$. Since
$w_\star^{(t)}$ minimizes $L(\cdot;\theta^{(t)},d^{(t)})$ over the convex set $\Wcal$ and
$w^{(t)}\in\Wcal$, the first-order optimality condition implies
\[
L(w^{(t)};\theta^{(t)},d^{(t)})
-
L(w_\star^{(t)};\theta^{(t)},d^{(t)})
\ge
\|w^{(t)}-w_\star^{(t)}\|_{\Sigma_{d^{(t)}}}^2
=
u_t^\top \Sigma_{d^{(t)}}u_t.
\]
By Assumption~\ref{assum:approx_est},
\[
u_t^\top \Sigma_{d^{(t)}}u_t \le \epsstat.
\]
Combining,
\[
|T_{2,t}|
\le
2\sqrt{\frac{|\Acal|\kappa}{1-\gamma}\,\epsstat}.
\]

Hence
\[
\mathrm{err}_t
\le
2\sqrt{\frac{|\Acal|}{1-\gamma}\left\|\frac{d^\star}{\nu}\right\|_\infty \epsapprox}
+
2\sqrt{\frac{|\Acal|\kappa}{1-\gamma}\,\epsstat}.
\]
This bound is uniform in $t$, so substituting into Lemma~\ref{lemma:npg_regret} gives the theorem.
Taking expectation preserves the bound.
\end{proof}

The theorem shows that the two sources of error enter in qualitatively different ways.

First, the approximation term is multiplied by the density ratio
\[
\left\|\frac{d^\star}{\nu}\right\|_\infty,
\]
which is a transfer term from the fitting distribution to the comparator distribution.
This is the natural price of model misspecification.

Second, the estimation term is controlled instead by the relative condition number $\kappa$,
which is a much milder coverage requirement. Intuitively, to estimate a linear predictor it is
enough that $\nu$ cover the relevant feature directions, rather than all state-action pairs
uniformly.

Finally, although $\kappa$ may depend on the feature geometry, it need not depend directly on
the size of the state space. In particular, one can often choose $\nu$ using only the feature set
so that $\kappa$ is controlled by a dimension-dependent quantity; we do not pursue this here.

\section{Bibliographic Remarks and Further Readings}\label{bib:pg_approx}

The notion of \emph{compatible function approximation} is due to
\cite{sutton1999policy}, which also proved the basic projection identity in
Lemma~\ref{lemma:compatible}. The close connection between compatible approximation
and the natural policy gradient, as in Lemma~\ref{eq:npg_argmin}, was noted in
\cite{Kakade01}.

The regret lemma (Lemma~\ref{lemma:npg_regret}) is closely related to mirror-descent
and online-learning analyses, going back to \cite{even-dar2009online}. The specific
performance guarantees in this chapter are derived from \cite{agarwal2019optimality}.
The $Q$-NPG update for log-linear policies is closely related to POLITEX
\cite{abbasi2019politex}. The same general perspective also applies to neural policy
classes; see \cite{agarwal2019optimality}. Related analyses of trust-region and natural
gradient methods for neural policies appear in \cite{caiTRPO,schulman2015trust}.

One of the main conceptual messages of this chapter is that direct policy optimization
can tolerate approximation error in a comparatively mild way. The approximation term
enters through a transfer factor to a \emph{fixed} comparator distribution, whereas
approximate value-function-based methods typically require much stronger control over
errors under a far larger family of distributions.
